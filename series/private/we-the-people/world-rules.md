# World Rules — We The People

## Historical fidelity rules

### What is fixed
- All documented historical events happen on their documented dates
- All historically attested facts about characters' lives, positions, and relationships are binding
- The outcomes of major events (battles, elections, legislative votes) are as recorded
- Characters cannot know things they historically did not know at the time of a scene

### What is licensed
- Inner life: thoughts, emotions, and motivations may be extrapolated from the documentary record (letters, diaries, speeches)
- Private conversations: dialogue in scenes without a documentary record may be invented, provided it is consistent with documented positions and speech patterns
- Sensory and atmospheric detail: weather, physical settings, clothing, food — use period-accurate research
- Minor undocumented interactions between historical figures who were in the same place

### What is prohibited
- Changing historical outcomes
- Having characters express views radically inconsistent with their documented positions without narrative justification
- Anachronistic language, concepts, or attitudes that belong to later eras
- Sanitizing the slavery question — it must be present wherever it was historically present

## The world of colonial and founding-era America

### Social structure
- Colonial America is rigidly hierarchical but with more permeability than England
- Gentlemen planters (Washington, Jefferson, Madison) vs. self-made merchants (Franklin) vs. working class (Paine)
- Women are legally subordinate — cannot vote, cannot own property in marriage, cannot hold office
- Enslaved people are property under law throughout the South and in northern cities
- Free Black people exist in the North but with severe restrictions
- Native peoples are present, politically significant, and largely absent from founders' documents

### Communication
- Letters are the primary medium of extended communication — weeks of transit time
- Newspapers are politically partisan and widely read; they shape public opinion
- Pamphlets are the medium for political argument (Common Sense is a pamphlet)
- Personal conversation at taverns, coffeehouses, and social gatherings drives political networks
- The Continental Congress operates by debate, committee, and consensus-building under time pressure

### Travel
- Boston to Philadelphia: approximately 7-10 days by post road (horse or carriage)
- Boston to London: 6-8 weeks by sea, weather-dependent
- Philadelphia to Virginia: 3-5 days by horse/carriage
- The ocean crossing is dangerous, cold, and miserable

### Economy
- Colonial economy is plantation-based in the South (tobacco, rice, indigo), trade-based in the North
- British mercantile system controls colonial trade; all trade must go through Britain
- Credit-based: planters like Jefferson are chronically in debt to British merchants
- Taxation without representation is not merely a slogan — colonial assemblies have no voice in Parliament

### Military
- Colonial militia: local, volunteer, poorly trained, unreliable under sustained pressure
- Continental Army: professional (ideally), underfunded, underequipped
- British Army: professional, well-equipped, but fighting 3,000 miles from home
- Naval power belongs entirely to Britain until the French alliance (1778)

## Series-level character rules

### Character consistency across books
- Documented positions and beliefs must remain consistent unless the character historically changed them
- Character relationships established in one book carry forward
- Deaths happen on historically documented dates
- Characters' documented speeches, letters, and writing may be quoted directly

### The founding contradictions — binding across all books
- Every major character who enslaved people must be depicted as doing so — it cannot be background noise
- The founding rhetoric of liberty and equality must exist in visible tension with the reality
- Women's exclusion from formal power must be legible — Abigail Adams's frustration is representative
- These are not defects to be excused but contradictions to be examined

## Anachronism watch list
These concepts did not exist in the founding era — avoid:
- "Democracy" as a positive term (founders feared direct democracy; they built a republic)
- "American" as a national identity (it forms during the period, use carefully)
- Political parties (emerge during Washington Administration)
- The word "politics" used casually (the founders used "politicks")
- Modern psychological vocabulary (no one in 1776 "processed trauma" or had "anxiety")
- Metric system, modern medicine concepts, modern communications
