# World Rules — Verdant Earth
*Permanent rules. Any book that contradicts these has an error.*

## The Ecological Covenant (2051)
- Global treaty following the Cascade Collapse of 2047–2049 (simultaneous failure of three major ocean current systems)
- Prohibits all new fossil fuel extraction; requires full phase-out by 2065
- Establishes Restoration Credits: economic units tied to measurable ecological recovery (carbon sequestration, biodiversity indices, ocean pH)
- Enforced by the Accord (successor to the UN, founded 2053)
- Not universally loved: some regions feel it was imposed by wealthy nations who caused the problem

## Governance: The Accord
- Voluntary cooperative of city-states and bioregional councils
- No standing army; enforcement is economic (credit sanctions)
- Three bodies: Technical Council (scientific standards), Trade Assembly (economic coordination), Rights Tribunal (human rights and ecological harm adjudication)
- Real power is often at the city-state and bioregional level

## Biotech Integration
- Living infrastructure is standard in Accord cities: mycorrhizal data networks (fungal fiber optics), biofilter wetlands treating water and air, mycelial composting systems integrated into building waste
- All biotech systems are certified by the Technical Council; uncertified modifications are illegal under the Covenant
- "Going wild" — running unsanctioned biotech — carries serious legal consequences
- Biotech systems are not infallible; they require maintenance, can be sabotaged, and sometimes fail in cascading ways

## Energy
- Decentralized renewable grid; no single point of failure by design
- Personal and community energy ownership is the norm (not utility monopolies)
- Energy is not scarce in Accord cities, but it is tracked (for Restoration Credit accounting)

## Technology
- AI is ubiquitous but narrow; general AI did not happen — systems are highly capable within domains but do not generalize
- No brain-computer interfaces (banned under the Rights Tribunal's 2068 Body Sovereignty ruling)
- Physical infrastructure has been significantly rebuilt; most 20th-century heavy industry has been replaced or converted

## What the Cascade Collapse left behind
- Sea level is 0.8m higher than 2000; many coastal cities rebuilt on elevated footings or with flood-adaptive design
- Several island nations are gone or greatly diminished; their populations resettled into coastal cooperatives
- Certain ecosystems (Great Barrier Reef, large sections of the Amazon) are in managed recovery but not restored
- A generation born after 2060 has never known a world without the Covenant; older generations remember the Before Time with a mix of grief and relief

## Economics
- Restoration Credits are the dominant global currency for large transactions; local currencies for daily commerce
- Intellectual property is governed by the Open Knowledge Compact (2058): most scientific and technical knowledge is freely accessible; commercial advantage comes from implementation, not information hoarding
- Scarcity economics still applies to physical goods and skilled labor; post-scarcity is not here yet

## NEW RULES ESTABLISHED — Book 1 (The Mycelial City)
*The following are now PROVEN, demonstrated facts about how this world's institutions and systems function. Any future book set in this world must reckon with them as precedent — not as untested theory.*

### The Open Knowledge Compact — now a PROVEN, demonstrated mechanism (not just a founding-era provision)
- The Compact (2058) grants a permanent, separate **public right of access** to ecological monitoring data — distinct from, and not routed through, any institution's internal reporting chain (not the incident queue, not the Technical Council, not any body that "can make a thing disappear").
- **Source**: Book 1, Chapters 4–6. Yara Osei used her personal Compact deposit credentials to publish the complete sabotage record directly to the public archive when every official channel had been shut to her.
- **What is now proven, on the page, for the first time in the series**: the mechanism *works exactly as designed* — independent strangers across the cooperative network began pulling and verifying the deposited record within nineteen minutes (the first reader was a researcher in the Nairobi bioregion); a "string of single, real, separate eyes" arrived independently at the same conclusion. A public deposit triggers an **automated, mandatory Technical Council notification** — "a mechanism [that] noticed on its own and... can't pretend it didn't" — which in turn triggered a real, fast, and largely uncontrollable institutional cascade (contract freezes, a formal certification review, and — within the same morning — a resignation).
- **Permanent precedent for future books**: this is no longer a theoretical "load-bearing founding commitment." It is now a *demonstrated, working, fast-acting* check on institutional capture — one that any future saboteur, corrupt official, or captured institution in this world must now treat as a known, provable risk. Likewise, any future protagonist locked out of official channels now has on-record proof that this door exists, has been used, and works.
- **Standing requirement** (now confirmed in practice): Compact deposit access is tied to *the work itself* — certified labor on shared environmental/public-infrastructure systems — not to any employer, assignment, or institutional standing. It is issued by the Covenant directly to the individual and cannot be revoked by an employer (an Authority suspension could not touch it).

### Credential / access-tier distinction — now fully established series-canon
- **Source**: Book 1, Chapters 4–6.
- New Accra's (and by extension, Accord-city) institutional access runs on at least two entirely separate credential systems with **separate failure modes**:
  1. **Authority/employer operational credentials** — assignment-based, can be suspended or frozen by institutional action (in Book 1, within four minutes of an attempted formal escalation, via a "unified registry" that links Authority and Accord secondment credentials together — one suspension action reached both Yara's and Del's access simultaneously).
  2. **Compact deposit / public-infrastructure-work credentials** — issued directly by the Covenant to individuals based on the nature of their work, entirely separate from any employer's authority to grant or revoke, and explicitly NOT reachable by an institutional suspension.
- This distinction is now load-bearing series-canon: future books can use either failure mode (or the gap between them) as a plot mechanism, but must not conflate the two systems or treat one as able to override the other.

### Manual biofilter override — now an established mechanical/procedural fact
- **Source**: Book 1, Chapter 6.
- Biofilter stations have a **manual override mechanism** that is purely mechanical, requires a physical key, and "answers to no credential at all." It requires only **"access and competence"** — meaning a suspended or credential-locked technician can still physically operate the system if they have the training and the access (which a suspension does not revoke, since station housings answer to badges/physical access, not operational assignment status).
- This establishes that New Accra's (and likely all Covenant-certified) biotech infrastructure has a deliberate **human-competence fallback layer** beneath its credentialed/automated systems — a designed redundancy, not a loophole. Future books should treat this as intentional resilience design baked into Covenant-era infrastructure.

### The suppression "kill threshold" — now an established ecological/technical rule
- **Source**: Book 1, Chapter 6.
- Biofilter colony suppression-response cannot be forced past a certain intensity without killing the colonies themselves — manual remediation must be paced to "what the living system can bear." Pushing the suppression dose too hard "overcorrects" and kills the very organisms being relied upon to fight the bloom.
- This is now a permanent rule of how mycorrhizal/biofilter biotech functions in this world: remediation is inherently rate-limited by the biology itself, not just by procedure or equipment. Any future book depicting biofilter remediation, sabotage, or recovery must respect this ceiling.

### Firmware/certification sabotage — a demonstrated method, now precedent
- **Source**: Book 1, Chapters 2–4.
- It is now established as *possible and proven* that a single, narrow, deliberate firmware modification can run identically across multiple manufacturers' hardware by operating at the monitoring-software layer (above and indifferent to the underlying machine) — meaning hardware diversity alone is NOT a defense against this class of sabotage.
- Such a modification can be installed during a legitimate-seeming certification site visit, with the certification itself serving as the cover and the paper trail. This makes the **certification process itself** a documented attack vector in this world — a permanent vulnerability class that future books may reference, reuse, or show being patched.
- The "selective substitution" technique (passing all data through accurately except one specific signal range, which is replaced with a synthesized nominal value) is now an established sabotage signature — subtle enough to look, on first inspection, like an aggregation/processing bug rather than deliberate interference.

## NEW RULES ESTABLISHED — Book 2 (Seed Vault)
*The following are now PROVEN, demonstrated facts about how this world's institutions and systems function. Any future book set in this world must reckon with them as precedent — not as untested theory.*

### The Genetic Diversity Preservation Council (GDPC) and the D-score system
- **Source**: Book 2, Chapters 2–6.
- The **Genetic Diversity Preservation Council (GDPC)** is an Accord body responsible for assessing the genetic-diversity significance of seed lots across the global cooperative seed network. It maintains the **D-score** ("diversity-significance scale") — a single number meant to capture how genetically irreplaceable a seed lot is, based on cold-tolerance/altitude-performance range, genetic rarity, and a "commercially equivalent varieties" comparison set.
- The D-score directly determines **monitoring frequency**: under **Protocol 4.7(c)**, lots with a D-score below a re-monitoring threshold (established in Book 2 as 2.5) are automatically exempted from mandatory re-testing at intermediate transit stations. This is an *automated* mechanism — no human signature required, and it operates "exactly as designed" even when its inputs are wrong.
- The GDPC's **2079 framework** established the comparison-set methodology currently in use. It was built by an eleven-person working group (six of whom did the actual technical drafting: three from commercial-crop cooperatives, one from a certification-readiness advisory body, two methodology-specialist statisticians with no agricultural affiliation). The founding survey underlying the comparison tables (2071–2076, forty-one bioregions) **never collected data from any bioregion above 2,000 meters** — not through deliberate exclusion, but because none of the survey's authors had contacts at high altitude.
- The framework underwent **one substantive review, in 2083** (the only review cycle it has ever had), and was **readopted unchanged in 2084**. No subsequent review cycle was scheduled or occurred — eleven years of institutional silence as of Book 2's events (2094).
- The **Comparison-Set Methodology Subpanel** (GDPC Technical Council, standing body, established 2080) is the body that currently maintains the framework. It has six members; as of Book 2, four hold professional/institutional ties to commercial-crop development programs, and two are methodology specialists without agricultural affiliation. One Subpanel member has fourteen years of continuous institutional involvement with the framework (originally an advisory-subcommittee signatory on the 2079 working group, now a sitting Subpanel member).
- **Demonstrated bias has two distinct, separately-confirmed components** (any future book referencing the D-score system must respect both):
  1. **Taxonomic/comparison-set bias** ("too tidy"): high-altitude Andean varietals are scored against a comparison set composed entirely of lowland commercial cultivars (e.g., Kennebec, Yukon Gold, Russet Burbank), producing systematically low scores (Q'asa Wasi's outgoing lots clustered 2.0–3.5 against an Atlantic circuit average of 5.2 and Mediterranean average of 5.1 for equivalent varietal classes).
  2. **Geographic bias** ("too narrow"): even the *same exact varietal* (andigenum) scores differently depending on which circuit/cooperative-of-origin it is routed through — confirmed example: 4.6 via the Mediterranean circuit vs. 2.3 via Q'asa Wasi, for the same varietal in a comparable harvest year under comparable testing conditions.
- **Permanent precedent for future books**: the D-score / Protocol 4.7(c) / Comparison-Set Methodology Subpanel system is now an established, demonstrated vector through which *institutional design* (not sabotage, not malice) can produce real, measurable ecological/genetic harm — "an incentive structure made visible," in Maialèn's phrase. Any future book may reference this system, show its review process playing out, or use the same structural pattern (a measurable score that gates monitoring/funding/attention, built from a dataset that excludes a category of place or people) elsewhere in the network.

### The Atlantic Seed Trust — registration and escalation conventions
- **Source**: Book 2, Chapters 1–6.
- The Atlantic Seed Trust is a cooperative seed-verification body (structurally similar to, but independent from, the Accord Technical Council) that employs cross-bioregional audit specialists. Auditors hold a personal **registration credential** (format confirmed: **AST-XXXX**, e.g., AST-7734) that is issued to the individual and persists independent of any specific field assignment — analogous to, but distinct from, the Compact deposit credentials established in Book 1 (a different institution, a similar "credential belongs to the person, not the assignment" structure).
- Escalation reference numbers follow the format **[initials]-[lot/site]-[year]-[code]** (e.g., MEE-441-2094-Q for Maialèn's initial escalation; MEE-2094-QW-04 for her field assignment).
- **The Trust's institutional default response pattern, now demonstrated twice in Book 2**: when an audit finding raises a question outside strict chain-of-custody verification (e.g., a methodology question), the Trust's standard response is to (1) acknowledge the finding as "noted" and "retained in the lot file," (2) state that the broader question is "a policy matter properly addressed through the [relevant body]'s annual submissions process — outside the scope of routine audit review," and (3) conclude "no further action is recommended/required at this time." This language pattern is now an established institutional voice/precedent — professionally correct, procedurally complete, and effectively a polite closure of any question the Trust does not want to own.
- When an auditor acts outside/beyond the scope of a closed assignment using their *personal* registration credential, the Trust's response is to place that registration **"under review pending consultation with the Compact's standards office"** — a new, open-ended institutional state, distinct from and more serious than a routine assignment closure, that does not resolve quickly (three weeks later, in Book 2, it had only softened to "under continued review, no further action required at this time").

### The Rights Tribunal — petition and administrative-hold mechanism
- **Source**: Book 2, Chapter 5.
- The Rights Tribunal (one of the Accord's three bodies, per the original World Rules — "human rights and ecological harm adjudication") accepts petitions from individuals with standing (a registration credential plus documented evidence) and from co-petitioning institutions (e.g., a cooperative council, represented by name).
- Petition reference numbers follow the format **RT-[year]-[number]** (e.g., RT-2094-11847).
- Petitions may be **"accepted for expedited review under Section 9"** of the Tribunal's procedural code, with a case officer assigned within five working days of acceptance.
- The Tribunal can issue an **administrative hold** — format **AH-[year]-[number]** (e.g., AH-2094-0231) — on a *specific session item* of another Accord body (in Book 2, a GDPC Technical Council session), postponing it "pending independent review," with no reschedule date required to be set. This is now an established, demonstrated mechanism: a Tribunal petition can functionally pause another body's regulatory process before the petition itself is substantively decided.
- **Permanent precedent for future books**: the Rights Tribunal is not merely an adjudicative body that rules on completed harms — it can intervene *procedurally*, in real time, to pause a pending institutional action while a petition is reviewed. This is a faster and more consequential mechanism than the Trust's internal escalation process, and is now a known tool available to any future protagonist with standing and documented evidence.

### The Compact — open-publication archive and citation visibility
- **Source**: Book 2, Chapter 5–6 (extends the Open Knowledge Compact mechanism established in Book 1).
- The Compact's open agricultural archive accepts deposits of historical/internal records (e.g., a cooperative's pre-Covenant handwritten ledgers, scanned and transcribed) and assigns a public reference number — format confirmed: **[SITE-CODE]-[CATEGORY]-[YEAR-RANGE]** (e.g., QW-GERM-2079-2093 for Q'asa Wasi's germination records spanning 1979–2093... or possibly representing the cooperative's record-keeping years; exact date-range semantics not fully specified in-text, but the format itself — site code, category code, year range — is established).
- Once deposited, an archive entry displays a **live citation/reference counter** visible to the depositor. In Book 2, this counter moved from 0 to 3 (overnight) to 14 (the next morning, across three independently-querying bioregions: Atlantic, Mediterranean, Andean) to 40+ (three weeks later) — entirely independent of any Tribunal or GDPC process.
- **Permanent precedent for future books**: this confirms and extends Book 1's finding that the Compact mechanism is fast-acting and effectively impossible to suppress once a deposit is made — but Book 2 adds that the *value* of a Compact deposit is not contingent on any other institutional process succeeding. Independent researchers across bioregions actively monitor for and use newly-available data ("there's a research group in the Atlantic bioregion that flags new high-altitude germination data automatically... they'll have a query running") — the archive is a living, queried resource, not a passive repository.

### Protocol 4.7(c) — automated monitoring-suspension mechanism
- **Source**: Book 2, Chapter 2.
- Formal rule, now established Covenant-level procedure: *"Lots with D-score below [re-monitoring] threshold are exempt from mandatory re-test at intermediate stations in standard-transit classification."* Triggers automatically, with a system-generated flag, no human signature. Operating exactly as designed is explicitly distinguished, in-text, from the mechanism being a "failure" — the flaw is upstream, in the D-score itself, not in Protocol 4.7(c)'s execution.
- This is now an established example of a broader pattern in this world's institutions: **automated mechanisms that function correctly can still produce systemic harm if their triggering inputs are miscalibrated** — directly parallel to, but structurally distinct from, Book 1's firmware-sabotage precedent (which required deliberate human action). Book 2's harm required no sabotage at all.

### The "2094" series timeline confirmed
- **Source**: Book 2, throughout (explicit dates: "RT-2094-11847," "MEE-2094-QW-04," "AH-2094-0231," etc.)
