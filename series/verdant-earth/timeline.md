# Series Timeline — Verdant Earth

## Before the Series: Key Historical Events

- **2047–2049**: The Cascade Collapse — simultaneous failure of the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation, parts of the Antarctic ice shelf, and three major monsoon systems. Mass displacement, crop failure, political collapse in several regions.
- **2051**: Ecological Covenant signed in Nairobi by 147 nations and city-states.
- **2053**: The Accord founded as successor governance body.
- **2057**: Yara Osei born in Kumasi Cooperative (Ghana).
- **2058**: Open Knowledge Compact; scientific data becomes a global commons.
- **2065**: Fossil fuel phase-out deadline reached in Accord territories.
- **2068**: Body Sovereignty ruling bans brain-computer interfaces.
- **2080s**: First generation raised entirely under the Covenant reaches adulthood; the "Covenant children."
- **2088**: New Accra (West African coast) achieves Restoration Credit surplus for the first time — a major symbolic milestone.
- **2091**: Book 1 begins.

## Book 1: The Mycelial City (2091)

### Background events established by Book 1 (occurred before the book's active timeline)
- **~6 years before Book 1 (~2085)**: Stations Seven and Eight installed with Cascavel Biotech monitoring hardware; Station Nine installed with Northbrook Biosystems hardware — a routine procurement-cycle difference later proven NOT to be the sabotage vector (both suppliers' equipment confirmed clean).
- **~2 years before Book 1 (~2089)**: Emil Strand, then Accord Technical Council Standards division, dismissed following "a standards disagreement that went the wrong way for him" — the kind of disagreement that happens to people who refuse to sign things they shouldn't.
- **~4 months before Book 1's active timeline (~early 2091)**: Altera Technical Services performs a single-day, three-station "recertification" of Stations Seven, Eight, and Nine — unusually fast for the scope — signed off by Emil Strand. During this site visit, sabotage firmware is physically installed on all three stations' monitoring nodes, masking elevated cyanobacteria readings and the colonies' true suppression-response above a specific threshold. Off-schedule, third-party-tagged entries are written into the stations' physical hardware logs (later found at Eight and Nine).
- **~47 days before Book 1's active timeline begins**: The masked cyanobacteria elevation pattern begins appearing in the raw data of Stations Seven, Eight, and Nine — well before the bloom would otherwise have been noticeable, meaning the suppression was installed *ahead of* the thing it was built to hide ("It was put in place ahead of the thing it's hiding").

### The active timeline — "The Bloom Clock" (4 days, ~114 hours total)
1. **Day 1, pre-dawn**: Yara Osei discovers the impossible reading at Station Seven (cyano elevation paired with flat coherence) and files Report EI-2091-447 to Director Nwosu. Independently, Del Marchetti (Accord Technical Council secondment) has been tracking the same anomaly from the Accord side.
2. **Day 1, that evening**: Report EI-2091-447 and its append-only intake receipt are both deleted from the Authority's systems — an act requiring Director-level access.
3. **Day 2**: Yara reports the deletion to Director Nwosu directly; Nwosu acknowledges the anomaly exists, claims it's under internal review, and reassigns Yara off the eastern inlet rotation ("conflict of interest"). Yara and Del compare data and establish the pattern goes back 47 days, not the 30 Yara had seen — meaning the suppression was deliberately pre-positioned. They calculate ~84 hours (±6) to chemical irreversibility.
4. **Day 3**: Yara discovers off-schedule, third-party-tagged firmware-sync entries in the physical hardware logs of Stations Eight and Nine. Del traces the matching certification reference numbers to Altera Technical Services, signed by Emil Strand. They prove the firmware performs "selective substitution" — passing all data through accurately except cyanobacteria readings above a threshold, which it replaces with synthesized nominal values — and that the same modification runs identically on two different manufacturers' hardware (ruling out a supply-chain vulnerability; proving the sabotage was deliberate and vector-specific to the certification visit). They discover Altera's sole director sits on the board of Rational Innovation's Infrastructure Advisory Group.
5. **Day 3, later**: Del identifies Rational Innovation's public position (certification has "calcified into obstruction") and its multi-jurisdiction reform campaign. He and Yara conclude the entire scheme is a "demonstration" — engineering a public, undeniable failure of New Accra's showcase certified biotech system to argue that certification itself is the problem, not a plot to poison the inlet for its own sake.
6. **Day 3, evening**: Yara and Del attempt the official escalation path (Del's formal Accord channel, with the complete evidentiary package). The response comes back in four minutes — both of their credentials are frozen/suspended ("conflict of interest") via the unified Authority/Accord registry. The official channel is proven to be closed to them.
7. **Day 4, pre-dawn (Hour ~92, ~22 hours from irreversibility)**: Yara fires her Open Knowledge Compact deposit — publishing the complete technical record (firmware delta, 47-day raw feed, lab assays, hardware-log photographs, certification records, and a carefully-sourced, non-speculative statement establishing that the deletion required Director-level access) directly to the public archive, bypassing every institutional channel that had been shut to her.
8. **Day 4, dawn (Hour ~92.3, within 19 minutes)**: Independent researchers and cooperatives across the network begin pulling and verifying the deposit unprompted — the record becomes permanently "out, real, seen," beyond recall. The Technical Council logs an automated, mandatory notification against the deposit. Del's frozen secondment is quietly restored overnight by parties "trying to get ahead of" the unfolding cascade.
9. **Day 4, morning (Hour ~94–99)**: Yara walks the manual biofilter suppression sequence by hand at Stations Seven through Nine, using a mechanical override key that "answers to no credential at all" — bypassing her suspension through competence rather than permission, paced carefully below the "kill threshold" so as not to overcorrect and kill the colonies. The bloom curve visibly begins to bend back under her hands.
10. **Day 4, midday (Hour ~99–100)**: Del reports the full institutional cascade to Yara at Station Seven: Altera's certification contracts frozen citywide pending investigation; the certification review formally opened, with Strand's signature inside the record under examination; Rational Innovation has issued a fast public denial (investigation continuing regardless); and **Director Nwosu has resigned**, stepping down before the review could reach her.
11. **Day 4, early afternoon (Hour ~100)**: The Technical Council's emergency response team arrives at the inlet — and finds containment already substantially achieved by Yara's manual work. The team lead confirms on-record that without this morning's intervention, "we'd be standing in a remediation that doesn't work."
12. **Day 4, late afternoon/evening (Hour ~104–106, ~8–10 hours of margin remaining)**: Yara returns alone to Station Seven, runs the same sensor check she ran on Day 1, and finds — for the first time in six days — that the smell and the readings finally agree. She writes the plain, true, factual log entry the entire crisis had been about being able to write. Book 1 ends here. **Chemical irreversibility (Hour 114) was never reached.**

## Ending state of the world at the close of Book 1
- The bloom is contained, with roughly 20 hours of margin preserved before the point of chemical irreversibility.
- The Open Knowledge Compact mechanism has been **publicly demonstrated to work** — fast, independent, and effectively impossible for any institution to suppress or outrun. This is now a known, provable capability of New Accra's (and the wider Covenant's) institutions.
- Altera Technical Services: all citywide certification contracts frozen, under formal investigation.
- The Technical Council's certification review is open and formal; Emil Strand's signature is inside it as evidence. The review's findings are explicitly placed months beyond the book's horizon.
- Rational Innovation has issued a public denial; the investigation continues regardless.
- **Director Adaeze Nwosu has resigned** — permanently departed from the Water Authority Directorship, under an officially ambiguous framing ("protected the process and the city's name") that the text deliberately never resolves into confession or exoneration.
- Yara Osei's Authority suspension remains formally in place but its practical force is broken; her reinstatement is in motion (not shown completing on-page).
- Del Marchetti's Accord secondment has been fully restored.
- The city of New Accra remains unaware, at the book's close, of how close it came to a public ecological failure — "it still believes in itself."

## Book 2: Seed Vault (2094) — COMPLETE

### Background events established by Book 2 (occurred before the book's active timeline)
- **1990, Q'asa Wasi**: "The resettlement" — the cooperative's official ledger was lost. (Exact nature/cause of the resettlement not detailed on-page; flagged as available history for a future book.)
- **1991, Q'asa Wasi**: Inés Huanca Quispe's grandmother began keeping the cooperative's records again, by hand, in pencil, in an unofficial notebook — "the worst years." First entry: a germination count for a tuber line, recorded with no expectation of an outside audience ("someone has to remember what grew here"). This entry is now part of the Compact archive deposit QW-GERM-2079-2093.
- **2079**: GDPC's comparison-set methodology framework adopted (the working group: eleven members, six drafters — three from commercial-crop cooperatives, one from a certification-readiness advisory body, two methodology-specialist statisticians; founding survey 2071–2076, forty-one bioregions, none above 2,000 meters).
- **2080**: GDPC Technical Council's Comparison-Set Methodology Subpanel established as a standing body.
- **2083**: GDPC opens its only methodology review to date. Q'asa Wasi's Seed Council submits three rounds of comments over the review period (the third including their own ledger data), all logged and acknowledged.
- **2084**: The 2079 framework is readopted unchanged; Q'asa Wasi's comments are not incorporated.
- **~11 years before Book 2 (~2083)**: (see above) — Inés has known about the D-score miscalibration for eleven years as of Book 2's events.
- **~Late 2093 (autumn close, Donostia)**: Maialèn certifies Lot 441 (*Solanum tuberosum* subsp. *andigenum*) at 91.00% viability before its transfer to Q'asa Wasi via Lisbon and Guayaquil intermediate stations.
- **~8 months before Book 2's active timeline**: Lot 441's D-score (2.1, below the 2.5 re-monitoring threshold) automatically triggers Protocol 4.7(c) at the Guayaquil intermediate station, suspending mandatory re-testing for the remainder of the lot's transit.
- **~2 years before Book 2's active timeline**: Doña Encarnación explains the D-score discrepancy to the Q'asa Wasi council after a younger council member asks why the cooperative's lots always score low.
- **September (autumn before Book 2)**: Lot 441 arrives at Q'asa Wasi; the cooperative's own technician records 91.00% viability on receipt — consistent with, but never independently re-derived from, the departure certificate.

### The active timeline — Day 1 through Day 12, plus a 3-week epilogue (October 2094)
1. **Day 1, Donostia**: Maialèn discovers that Lot 441's transfer record shows four identical 91.00% viability readings across eleven months and three independent testing events (departure, Lisbon retest, Guayaquil retest, arrival) — "a number someone wrote down," not a number that traveled. She and Bixente discuss; she departs for Q'asa Wasi, arriving via Lima and Cusco.
2. **Day 1 (arrival), Q'asa Wasi**: Inés Huanca Quispe receives Maialèn personally. Maialèn verifies Q'asa Wasi's own receipt documentation for Lot 441 — finds it complete and accurate. Requests the monitoring-cycle records.
3. **Day 2, Q'asa Wasi**: Inés produces the monitoring-cycle record showing Protocol 4.7(c)'s automated suspension flag (D-score 2.1 vs. threshold 2.5), dated eight months prior. Maialèn examines the D-score calculation sheet and identifies the comparison-set problem (the "commercially equivalent varieties" field lists six lowland cultivars, none above 3,500m, none Andean).
4. **Day 2–3, Q'asa Wasi**: Maialèn cross-references all 43 of Q'asa Wasi's outgoing lot D-scores from the past five years (clustered 2.0–3.5) against Atlantic (5.2) and Mediterranean (5.1) circuit averages for equivalent varietal classes. Files escalation MEE-441-2094-Q. The Trust responds within 16 hours: monitoring-gap "noted and retained in the lot file"; methodology question is "outside the scope of routine audit review... no further action is recommended at this time."
5. **Day 3, Q'asa Wasi**: Maialèn asks Inés for access to the cooperative's pre-Covenant ledgers. Inés defers, asking Maialèn instead to spend the next day working the tuber harvest on the Long Terraces.
6. **Day 4, Q'asa Wasi**: Maialèn works the harvest with Rosalía's crew and meets Doña Encarnación ("The paper says what the paper was given. I know what this plant does." — 41 years on the same tuber line). That evening, Inés opens the locked records cabinet: pre-Covenant ledgers going back generations. Maialèn cross-references fifteen *andigenum* lots over nine years — germination/viability consistently in the high-80s/low-90s — against D-scores of 2.0–3.5. Inés reveals the council has known about this gap for **eleven years** (since the 2083 GDPC review) and submitted three rounds of unincorporated comments.
7. **Day 5–7, Q'asa Wasi**: Maialèn researches the GDPC's 2079 framework's design history overnight — identifies the eleven-person working group, six drafters (three commercial-crop-cooperative-affiliated, one certification-advisory-affiliated, two statisticians), and the founding survey's total absence of high-altitude data.
8. **Day 8, Q'asa Wasi**: Bixente arrives (having completed the southern circuit). Reviews all of Maialèn's findings; identifies the **geographic bias** — the same andigenum varietal scores 4.6 via the Mediterranean circuit vs. 2.3 via Q'asa Wasi. Together they identify the **Comparison-Set Methodology Subpanel** (GDPC Technical Council, est. 2080, six current members, four commercial-crop-tied, one with fourteen years of continuous involvement). One Subpanel member's Mediterranean-basin consortium affiliation triggers Bixente's unconfirmed half-memory of "that business in New Accra." Maialèn asks Inés whether Q'asa Wasi will co-file a formal methodology challenge; Inés takes the question to the full council (terrace representatives and elders included).
9. **Day 8 (overnight)–Day 9**: The council votes 8–3 to co-file (Tomás among the three dissenters, voicing the institutional-retaliation risk on record before the vote). Inés and Tomás bring the council's authorization to Maialèn.
10. **Day 9, Q'asa Wasi (mid-morning)**: The Trust formally closes Maialèn's field assignment (MEE-2094-QW-04) — "no evidence of fraudulent record-keeping... assignment is considered complete... file your interim report... within seven days as a formality." The methodology question is again called "a policy matter... outside the scope of routine audit review."
11. **Days 9–11, Q'asa Wasi**: Maialèn (drafting) and Bixente (fact-checking) build the four-part methodology challenge document: (1) the comparison-set/taxonomic mismatch, (2) Protocol 4.7(c)'s monitoring-suppression chain, (3) the fifteen-lot ledger cross-reference and the geographic gap, (4) a timeline naming the Comparison-Set Methodology Subpanel by name and composition. They discover the GDPC Technical Council session presenting the Subpanel's codification recommendation is **eleven days out** (later, nine days from drafting). The pre-Covenant ledgers (fifteen lots, nine years) are deposited to the Compact's open archive as **QW-GERM-2079-2093** — citation count 0 → 1 (19 minutes) → 3 (overnight).
12. **Day 12, dawn, Q'asa Wasi (Nursery Terraces)**: The Lot 441 backup-stock germination trial returns **78% viability against the certified 91%** — a confirmed 13-point loss. The cooperative mobilizes its own backup stock, redistributing it across three terraces within the hour (some of it later sent to a cooperative two valleys over).
13. **Day 12, 04:00, Q'asa Wasi (records room)**: Maialèn files a Rights Tribunal petition (**Ref. RT-2094-11847**) under her own personal credentials (AST-7734), co-petitioned by the Q'asa Wasi Cooperative Seed Council (represented by Inés Huanca Quispe), attaching the four-part methodology document, the council's authorization, the ledger archive reference, and a request for expedited review given the nine-day window before the GDPC session.
14. **Day 12, dawn (Watching Wall)**: The Tribunal's Notice of Acceptance arrives — petition accepted for expedited review under Section 9, case officer within five working days; **administrative hold (Notice AH-2094-0231)** issued on the GDPC Technical Council Subpanel's codification session, postponed indefinitely. Citation count on QW-GERM-2079-2093 climbs from 3 to 14 overnight (Atlantic, Mediterranean, and Andean bioregions independently querying).
15. **Day 12, mid-morning**: The Trust places Maialèn's registration (AST-7734) "under review pending consultation with the Compact's standards office," as a direct consequence of the Tribunal filing.
16. **Day 12, midday (Long Terraces)**: Planting day. Maialèn learns the foot-plow from Rosalía and Doña Encarnación. Inés tells her that a cooperative two valleys over — whose D-score suppression went on longer — has lost its heritage oca line entirely (backup stock <30% viability); the line is being outcrossed with a related variety using some of Q'asa Wasi's donated surplus. Maialèn names this "the difference between a loss and an ending."
17. **Day 12, late afternoon (records room)**: Maialèn writes her formal closing audit report for MEE-2094-QW-04 — three findings (Protocol 4.7(c) operated as designed; the D-score comparison-set methodology is the structural flaw, fifteen years uncorrected; the Lot 441 trial is confirmed material harm) plus one open question (whether the eventual review examines only the Subpanel's composition or the broader geographic-bias pattern network-wide). Signed under her own name and registration number.
18. **Day 12, evening (Watching Wall)**: Inés tells Maialèn the story of her grandmother's 1991 ledger entry. The Tuesday supply truck arrives.
19. **Three weeks later, Donostia (epilogue)**: The Tribunal review remains in early stages (case officer assigned as promised; a documentation request answered same-day). The GDPC Subpanel's session remains undated. The QW-GERM-2079-2093 citation count has passed 40. Maialèn's registration status has softened to "under continued review, no further action required at this time" but remains open. Maialèn rereads the original Lot 441 transfer record and writes, in the margin she'd left blank at the start: *"Whose claim? Mine now too. I signed it forward. — M.E.G."*

## Ending state of the world at the close of Book 2
- Lot 441's monitoring gap is explained (Protocol 4.7(c) operating as designed on a miscalibrated D-score) and on record; the 13-point viability loss is confirmed and absorbed by Q'asa Wasi's backup stock.
- The Q'asa Wasi Cooperative Seed Council is, for the first time in its history, formally on record as co-petitioner in a Rights Tribunal challenge (RT-2094-11847) to a GDPC methodology — an 8–3 council vote, with the dissent's institutional-risk warning explicitly acknowledged rather than dismissed.
- The GDPC Technical Council's Comparison-Set Methodology Subpanel session (codification vote on the 2079 framework) is under an indefinite administrative hold (AH-2094-0231) — no reschedule date as of three weeks later. The Subpanel's six-member composition (four commercial-crop-tied, one with fourteen years of continuity) is unchanged.
- Q'asa Wasi's pre-Covenant germination ledgers (QW-GERM-2079-2093) are permanently and publicly archived via the Compact, with a citation count past 40 and climbing, across at least three bioregions (Atlantic, Mediterranean, Andean).
- Maialèn Etxeberria's registration (AST-7734) carries an open, unresolved institutional question — "under continued review, no further action required at this time" — three weeks after she filed a Tribunal petition under her own credentials, beyond her employer's endorsement.
- A cooperative "two valleys over" from Q'asa Wasi has lost its heritage oca line (an "ending," not a "loss") and is outcrossing its remnant stock with Q'asa Wasi's donated surplus — outcome unresolved.
- The "New Accra" thread (Bixente's half-memory of a Mediterranean-basin consortium departure, possibly connected to a current Subpanel member) remains entirely unconfirmed.
