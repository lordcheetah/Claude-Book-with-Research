## Yara Osei
*(protagonist of Book 1: The Mycelial City)*

### Permanent facts
- Age at series start: 34 (born 2057, grew up in the resettlement generation)
- Background: Born in the Kumasi Cooperative to parents who relocated from flooded coastal areas; grew up with the Covenant as simply the way the world worked
- Expertise: Water systems engineering; specializes in biofilter networks
- Core traits: Methodical, skeptical of easy answers, protective of the systems she maintains, dry humor under pressure

### Per-book arc summary
- **Book 1 (The Mycelial City)**: Yara discovered that Station Seven's biofilter readings were being deliberately falsified by modified certification firmware, traced the sabotage (with Del Marchetti) to Altera Technical Services and, behind it, the Technist advocacy group Rational Innovation — who wanted New Accra's showcase biofilter system to fail publicly as an argument for deregulating biotech certification. When her report was deleted from an append-only system and she was suspended on a "conflict of interest" pretext (within four minutes of attempting to escalate), she used her personal Open Knowledge Compact deposit credentials — separate from, and untouchable by, the Water Authority — to publish the complete technical record directly to the public archive. The deposit triggered an independent verification cascade and a fast institutional response that the internal channels had been built to outpace. She then walked the manual biofilter suppression sequence by hand, station by station, using a mechanical override key that "answers to no credential at all," and contained the bloom roughly 20 hours ahead of the point of chemical irreversibility — before the Council's powered emergency team even arrived. **Permanent changes**: Completed her core arc from "the systems are enough" to "the systems need defending" (see series-level arc, now confirmed). Ended the book with her Authority suspension still formally in place but its practical force broken, reinstatement underway, and her professional standing transformed — she is now the engineer who forced an entire institution to look at itself and was right. Her relationship with Director Nwosu ended permanently (see Nwosu entry). Deepened into a settled, wordless full-trust partnership with Del Marchetti — explicitly framed as the mature form of their relationship, restrained rather than romantic. Demonstrated, on-page and for the series' future reference, that her core methods (smell-before-numbers, physical samples, paper notebook, local off-network copies, reading everything three times) are not just personal quirks but load-bearing professional discipline that wins against institutional sabotage.

### Series-level arc
Yara's story is about what it means to protect something worth protecting — and what you sacrifice to do it. **Book 1 confirms and completes this trajectory's first turn**: she began believing that good technical work, honestly done, would be protected by the institutions built to protect it; she ends knowing that the systems need active defending, not just maintaining — and that defending them sometimes means using the part of the system the institutions themselves forgot was still theirs to use against them (the Compact). Future books should treat her as someone who has already paid this particular cost and crossed this particular threshold — she will not be naive about institutional protection again, but she has not become cynical either; her competence and care for the systems she maintains remain her core identity.

## Del Marchetti
*(supporting character, Book 1)*

### Permanent facts
- Age at series start: 41
- Background: Italian-Brazilian, grew up in a Technist-leaning family; chose the cooperative path as an adult and has never fully shaken the sense of being a convert
- Role: Systems analyst, Accord Technical Council secondment in New Accra
- Core traits: Verbose, politically aware, genuinely committed but not naive about institutions

### Per-book arc summary
- **Book 1 (The Mycelial City)**: Del independently noticed the same masked-data pattern from the Accord's side and brought it to Yara; the two formed a full investigative partnership. He provided the institutional fluency that let them trace Altera → Rational Innovation and correctly read the sabotage as "a demonstration, not destruction" — a play to make the careful system fail publicly so its caution could be discredited. **Permanent changes**: His Technist-adjacent past (see "Connection to Rational Innovation" in his project bible) surfaced on-page for the first time — he recognized Rational Innovation's talking points by heart because he had heard them for years in his twenties, before he chose the cooperative path. This was deliberately NOT fully confessed or explained; it closed in Book 1 as "a small, visible settling... an account she had never seen the opening of, closing now in the ordinary afternoon," with Del explicitly stating he is now "content to let" the remaining institutional threads (the Altera-board director's identity) go to the Council's investigation rather than chase them himself — "that's new for me. I used to want to chase those all the way down myself." His Accord secondment credentials were frozen alongside Yara's suspension (same unified registry, same four-minute response) and were quietly restored overnight by someone "trying to get ahead of" the unfolding cascade — a detail establishing that institutional actors inside the Accord/Authority structure were aware of and reactive to the deposit before any formal process began. Ended the book fully reinstated, beginning "the long unglamorous business of being reinstated" in an official capacity, and in a settled, mature partnership with Yara.

### Series-level arc note (new)
Del's "Technist background available-but-unconfirmed" status is now closed for Book 1's purposes but left as available territory: the text is explicit that something in him "settled" and an account "closed," but the specifics of what he did, said, or was part of in his Technist-adjacent years were never confessed on-page. A sequel could, in principle, reopen this — but doing so would need new narrative justification; Book 1 treats it as a completed, restrained resolution, not a loose thread.

## The Accord Technical Council
*Institution — recurring background presence*

- The standards body for all biotech certification in Accord cities
- Tends toward conservatism: slow to certify new systems, fast to investigate failures
- Has genuine scientific integrity but is susceptible to political pressure from major city-states
- First appears in Book 1 as the body that must certify Yara's findings

## Director Adaeze Nwosu
*(introduced Book 1 — now departed; permanent series-canon as a closed account)*

### Permanent facts
- Ran the New Accra Water Authority's technical division for twenty years; "two people in one office — the engineer who could still read a sensor log faster than half the staff, and the operator who knew exactly which council member to call."
- **PERMANENT STATUS: RESIGNED.** Stepped down from the Directorship the same morning the Compact deposit's institutional cascade began — before the formal review could reach her. This is now a permanent fact of New Accra's institutional history; she is no longer Water Authority Director and the text gives no indication she will return to public office.
- **The official framing of her departure** (now part of New Accra's institutional record): she "protected the process and the city's name" — held back an urgent flag and let internal review run rather than raise the alarm, judged it her call to make. Nothing in the formal record states she participated in the sabotage.
- **Permanently and deliberately UNRESOLVED**: whether her actions constituted an honest (if catastrophic) misjudgment, reputation protection, or self-protection remains explicitly ambiguous. She never confessed; the text is explicit that "the shape was all the answer there would ever be." Her own sentence — "I told myself —" — was left permanently unfinished. Any future book that wants to revisit her must treat this ambiguity as the canonical state, not retcon a clean answer into it.
- Her final on-page words to Yara, unfinished: "I told myself —"
- Yara's eulogy for her, delivered to Del at the inlet: "She read a log faster than anyone I worked under." This is the only epitaph she receives on-page.

## Emil Strand
*(introduced Book 1 — OPEN THREAD, deliberately unresolved; strong Book 2 candidate)*

### Permanent facts
- Former Accord Technical Council, Standards division. Dismissed roughly two years before Book 1's events, following "a standards disagreement that went the wrong way for him" — the kind of disagreement that happens to people who refuse to sign things they shouldn't.
- Signed the clean recertification of Stations Seven, Eight, and Nine (the certification that covered Altera's installation of the masking firmware) roughly four months before Book 1's events — a single-day, three-station certification that was unusually fast.
- **OPEN THREAD — DO NOT RESOLVE WITHOUT NEW NARRATIVE JUSTIFICATION**: His negligence-vs-complicity status is explicitly and permanently unresolved at Book 1's close. Del's framing stands as the last word given: "He either tested the masked signature range and missed it, or he tested it and signed anyway... I don't know which. We may never know which." His signature is now formally inside the Technical Council's certification review — "not our accusation. Their evidence now" — but the review's findings are explicitly placed months beyond Book 1's horizon.
- A fired Council insider with a grievance against the certification system who ended up doing certification work for the firm that sabotaged the system he was fired from defending — establishes that Rational Innovation's network "runs through Council connections, not just contracting firms." This fact (the *connective-tissue* significance of his role) is permanent regardless of how his personal culpability resolves.

## Altera Technical Services
*(institution/entity — introduced Book 1; now under permanent formal cloud)*

### Permanent facts
- Single-director certified technical contractor; performed the four-month-old recertification of Stations Seven, Eight, and Nine that covered the installation of sabotage firmware.
- Its sole director holds a board seat on Rational Innovation's Infrastructure Advisory Group — establishing a documented, on-the-record corporate link between a certification contractor and a Technist deregulation-advocacy organization.
- **PERMANENT STATUS: ALL CITYWIDE CERTIFICATION CONTRACTS FROZEN, pending formal investigation** — announced the same morning the Compact deposit went public. This is now a fact of New Accra's institutional landscape: Altera cannot currently certify anything in the city, and its fate is entirely in the Technical Council's hands.
- The firm's modus operandi (a "surgeon's modification, not a vandal's" — installing a single, narrow, deliberate firmware substitution that ran identically across two different hardware manufacturers' equipment because it operated at the monitoring-software layer, indifferent to the underlying machine) is now a documented sabotage method in the world — any future contractor-based sabotage should be measured against or distinguished from this precedent.

## Rational Innovation (Infrastructure Advisory Group)
*(institution/entity — introduced Book 1; now under formal investigation, OPEN THREAD on leadership)*

### Permanent facts
- A Technist-leaning advocacy organization; runs roughly six advisory bodies, the Infrastructure Advisory Group among them. Publishes studies, funds reports, places people on advisory panels.
- **Their stated public position** (now permanent series-canon and worth tracking for consistency): that Covenant-era biotech certification "has calcified into obstruction," that caution which made sense early now costs more than it protects, and that certification reform (faster pathways, lower independent-verification thresholds, more weight on contractor self-certification) would serve the public better. Del's assessment, now canonical: "It's not a stupid argument and it's not a cartoon... that's what makes it dangerous."
- Has been actively pushing certification-reform proposals (tailored per-jurisdiction) in **three jurisdictions**, one of which is New Accra — chosen specifically because New Accra is the Accord's flagship "proof that the careful way works."
- **The demonstrated play, now permanent series history**: Rational Innovation's apparent strategy was not to cause ecological harm for its own sake but to engineer a *public, undeniable failure of a showcase certified system* — to convert "the certified biotech failed" into an argument that certification itself is the problem. Del's summary, now canonical: "The sabotage isn't destruction. It's a demonstration."
- **PERMANENT STATUS at Book 1's close**: issued a fast, professionally lawyered public denial ("no involvement, no knowledge, full cooperation") within hours of the cascade beginning. The Technical Council's investigation continues regardless — explicitly stated to be untouched by the denial.
- **OPEN THREAD — the cleanest sequel hook in the book**: the identity of the Altera board member / Rational Innovation director who authorized the connection is explicitly UNCONFIRMED at Book 1's close. Del hands this off on-page to the Technical Council's investigation ("a name for the Council's investigation now... subpoena power, financial records, the whole apparatus we don't have and shouldn't try to be"). This is the most natural inciting thread for a Book 2 institutional-conspiracy arc.
