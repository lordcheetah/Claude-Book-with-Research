## Book 1: The Mycelial City
- **Status**: COMPLETE (6 chapters, fully reviewed and archived)
- **Project folder**: `projects/verdant-earth-book-1`
- **Time period covered**: 2091, New Accra (4-day active timeline, "the Bloom Clock")
- **Central question**: Who is sabotaging the biofilter network, and why?
- **Answer the book provides**: Altera Technical Services, a single-director certification contractor, installed sabotage firmware in three estuary biofilter stations during a cover certification visit (signed by fired ex-Council analyst Emil Strand). Behind Altera stands Rational Innovation's Infrastructure Advisory Group — a Technist deregulation-advocacy organization whose actual goal was not ecological harm for its own sake, but to engineer a *public, undeniable failure* of New Accra's showcase certified biotech system, in order to argue that Covenant-era certification itself is the obstruction. "The sabotage isn't destruction. It's a demonstration."

- **What it permanently established** (series-canon — see characters.md, world-rules.md, timeline.md for full detail):
  - **The Open Knowledge Compact mechanism is now PROVEN IN PRACTICE**, not just a theoretical Covenant-era right: it fired, was independently verified by strangers within nineteen minutes, triggered an automated mandatory Technical Council notification, and produced a fast, real institutional cascade (contract freezes, formal certification review, a resignation) — all within a single morning, faster than any internal channel had moved in the entire book. This is now a demonstrated capability of New Accra's (and the Covenant's) institutions that any future book must reckon with as precedent.
  - **The credential/access-tier distinction** — Authority/employer operational credentials (assignment-based, suspendable by institutional action) vs. Compact deposit/public-infrastructure-work credentials (issued directly by the Covenant to individuals, untouchable by any employer) — is now fully established, load-bearing series-canon with two clearly separate failure modes.
  - **The manual biofilter override mechanism**: mechanical, physical-key-based, credential-free, requiring only "access and competence" — an intentional human-competence fallback layer built into Covenant-era biotech infrastructure.
  - **The suppression "kill threshold"**: biofilter remediation is rate-limited by the biology itself — pushing the suppression dose too hard kills the colonies being relied upon to fight the bloom. A permanent ecological/technical rule for any future biofilter remediation scenes.
  - **A demonstrated sabotage method**: a single, narrow firmware modification (selective substitution of one signal range) that runs identically across multiple hardware manufacturers by operating at the monitoring-software layer — proving that hardware diversity alone isn't a defense, and that the certification process itself is a viable attack vector in this world.
  - **Character-permanent outcomes**: Director Adaeze Nwosu resigned (no longer Water Authority Director — permanent departure, officially ambiguous framing, deliberately never resolved into confession or exoneration); Altera's certification contracts frozen citywide and under formal investigation; Yara Osei and Del Marchetti both have institutional reinstatement underway/complete; Yara's series-level arc (systems-are-enough → systems-need-defending) is now confirmed and complete for this book.

- **Ending state**: The bloom is contained with ~20 hours of margin preserved before chemical irreversibility. Altera frozen and under investigation; the Technical Council's certification review is open and formal with Strand's signature inside it; Rational Innovation has issued a public denial (investigation continuing regardless); Nwosu has resigned; Yara's suspension is practically broken and reinstatement is in motion; Del's secondment is fully restored; the city of New Accra remains unaware of how close it came to public ecological failure.

- **Threads left open** (deliberate sequel hooks, explicitly handed to the Technical Council's ongoing investigation on-page — NOT loose ends):
  1. **Emil Strand's negligence-vs-complicity status** — a name on a signature now inside a formal review; the book explicitly declines to resolve whether he knew what he was certifying.
  2. **The identity of the Altera board member / Rational Innovation director** who authorized the connection — explicitly unconfirmed, formally handed off to the Council's investigation with subpoena power neither protagonist has. The single cleanest inciting-question candidate for a Book 2 institutional-conspiracy arc.
  3. (Minor, bounded) **Del Marchetti's Technist-era background** — received a small, deliberately incomplete, never-fully-confessed closing beat ("an account... closing... in the ordinary afternoon"); available-but-unconfirmed territory for a sequel, would need new narrative justification to reopen.
  4. The certification review's eventual findings and accountability outcomes — explicitly placed "months" beyond the book's horizon, entirely off-page.

## Book 2: Seed Vault
- **Status**: COMPLETE (6 chapters, fully reviewed and archived)
- **Project folder**: `projects/verdant-earth-book-2`
- **Time period covered**: October 2094, Q'asa Wasi Cooperative (Cusco bioregion, Peru, ~3,800m) — Day 1 through Day 12, plus a 3-week epilogue in Donostia. Confirms the series' "3 years after Book 1" gap (Book 1 = 2091).
- **Protagonist**: Maialèn Etxeberria Goikoetxea, a cross-bioregional seed-lot audit specialist for the Atlantic Seed Trust, based in Donostia (Basque coast). Yara Osei and Del Marchetti do not appear; New Accra is referenced only via an unconfirmed half-memory (see "New Accra thread" below).
- **Central question**: Why does Lot 441's viability certificate show the exact same 91.00% reading across four checkpoints and eleven months of transit — and what does that "too clean" stability actually mean?
- **Answer the book provides**: The lot was never re-tested in transit. An automated mechanism (Protocol 4.7(c)) suspends mandatory re-testing for any lot whose D-score falls below a threshold (2.5) — and Lot 441's D-score (2.1) was itself the product of a fifteen-year-old GDPC comparison-set methodology (the 2079 framework) that classifies high-altitude Andean varietals against a comparison set of lowland commercial cultivars exclusively, because the founding survey never collected data above 2,000 meters. Working with Inés Huanca Quispe and the Q'asa Wasi Seed Council — who had identified and reported this exact problem eleven years earlier, to no effect — Maialèn and Bixente Agirre also identified a second, geographic layer of bias (the same varietal scores differently depending on cooperative of origin, not just varietal class). "The methodology isn't lying... It's too *tidy*... It's too *narrow*."

- **What it permanently established** (series-canon — see characters.md, world-rules.md, timeline.md for full detail):
  - **The GDPC, the D-score system, and Protocol 4.7(c)** are now established Covenant-level institutions/mechanisms: a diversity-significance score that gates monitoring frequency via an automated suspension protocol, currently miscalibrated for high-altitude varietals along two distinct, separately-confirmed axes (taxonomic/comparison-set bias and geographic/origin bias). The **Comparison-Set Methodology Subpanel** (GDPC Technical Council, est. 2080, six members, four commercial-crop-tied) is now a named, recurring institutional body.
  - **The Atlantic Seed Trust's institutional voice** is established precedent: a specific "noted, retained, outside the scope of routine audit review, no further action recommended" response pattern to escalations the Trust does not want to own — now demonstrated twice in this book.
  - **The Rights Tribunal's procedural powers** are extended beyond Book 1's adjudicative role: petitions can be "accepted for expedited review under Section 9," and the Tribunal can issue an **administrative hold** on another Accord body's pending session/vote — a real-time procedural intervention, not just a post-hoc ruling. Reference-number formats established: RT-[year]-[number] for petitions, AH-[year]-[number] for administrative holds.
  - **The Compact's open archive** (extending Book 1's mechanism) now has a confirmed reference-number format (SITE-CATEGORY-YEARRANGE, e.g., QW-GERM-2079-2093) and a demonstrated, visible citation/reference counter that climbs rapidly and independently once data becomes available — confirming the archive is actively monitored by researchers across bioregions, not just a passive repository.
  - **Atlantic Seed Trust registration credentials** (format AST-XXXX) are confirmed as personal, persistent, and separable from any specific assignment — but using one beyond an employer's endorsement can trigger an open-ended institutional review of the credential itself.
  - **Character-permanent outcomes**: Maialèn Etxeberria filed a Tribunal petition under her own credentials, co-petitioned by the Q'asa Wasi Seed Council (an 8–3 council vote); her registration (AST-7734) now carries unresolved institutional scrutiny. The Q'asa Wasi cooperative is now formally on record, by name, in an Accord-level institutional challenge for the first time in its history. Inés Huanca Quispe, Bixente Agirre, Tomás, and Doña Encarnación are established recurring/available characters (see characters.md).

- **Ending state**: Lot 441's monitoring gap is explained and its 13-point viability loss confirmed and absorbed via Q'asa Wasi's backup-stock mobilization. The Tribunal petition (RT-2094-11847) is accepted for expedited review, with a case officer assigned; the GDPC Subpanel's codification session is under an indefinite administrative hold (AH-2094-0231). The QW-GERM-2079-2093 archive citation count has passed 40 across at least three bioregions. Maialèn's registration status (AST-7734) is "under continued review, no further action required at this time" — open but quiet. A cooperative two valleys over has lost its heritage oca line entirely and is outcrossing its remnant stock with Q'asa Wasi's donated surplus.

- **Threads left open** (deliberate sequel hooks — NOT loose ends):
  1. **The Rights Tribunal's eventual findings (RT-2094-11847)** — explicitly a years-away question; the substantive outcome and its implications for the comparison-set framework are entirely off-page.
  2. **Maialèn's registration status (AST-7734)** — open-ended; could resolve quietly into nothing, or escalate into a formal conduct review of her filing under personal credentials.
  3. **Geographic scope of the D-score bias beyond Q'asa Wasi** — explicitly the audit report's open question; whether the eventual review examines only the Subpanel's current composition or the broader network-wide pattern. The accelerating citation count (3→14→40+) is suggestive but not confirmatory.
  4. **The "New Accra" thread** — Bixente's unconfirmed half-memory: one of the six current Comparison-Set Methodology Subpanel members has a Mediterranean-basin consortium affiliation and "left a consortium post a few years back, around the time of that business in New Accra." Entirely unconfirmed; lowest-priority but a potential bridge back to Book 1's Rational Innovation/Altera network.
  5. **The cooperative two valleys over** — its heritage oca line is lost; the outcrossing effort with Q'asa Wasi's donated surplus and the cooperative's longer-term recovery are unresolved.
