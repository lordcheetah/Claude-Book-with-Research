# Station Alpha

## Overview
- Type: Deep-water processing and logistics station
- Importance: Primary setting — 90% of the story takes place here

---

## Physical description

### General layout
A converted deep-sea platform, Station Alpha sits 80 kilometers offshore in what used to be international waters (the designation is now contested). Fifteen decks, most of them below the surface. The upper three decks — administration, residential, and the operations floor — are above water. Below: processing facilities, storage, the maintenance warrens, and the data center.

The station was built in two phases: the original industrial shell (visible in the exposed structural steel and mismatched panel colors) and the corporate retrofit (clean surfaces, directional lighting, the constant low hum of climate control). The two layers never fully resolved.

### Key areas
- **Operations floor (Deck 2)**: Open-plan, wall-to-wall displays, the heart of data flow. Maya's workspace. Deliberately designed to feel transparent — no private conversations.
- **The Warrens (Decks 9–12)**: Maintenance access corridors. Low ceilings, service pipes, emergency lighting. Gets genuinely dark. Workers on these decks develop a particular navigational fluency.
- **Medical bay (Deck 5)**: Smells chemical. Staffed by one physician, two techs. Handles industrial injury more than illness.
- **The Hub (Deck 3)**: Cafeteria, common area, the social center. Conversations here travel fast.
- **Director's level (Deck 1, restricted access)**: Vasquez's offices and the executive systems room. Maya has never been inside.
- **Data center (Deck 11)**: Cold, loud with server fans. Access logs everything. The place where the real answers are stored.

### Hidden elements
- **Secondary data cache (Deck 13)**: Appears in maintenance schematics as "environmental monitoring." Actual purpose unknown until Act 2B.
- **Emergency evacuation shaft E-7**: Runs from Deck 1 to Deck 13 without triggering standard movement logs — designed for emergencies, occasionally used for other purposes.

## Atmosphere

### Default mood
Functional isolation. The station is efficient, well-maintained, and subtly oppressive. People are professional; friendships are real but calibrated. Everyone knows they're temporary — contract workers rotating in and out. The station outlasts all of them.

### Sensory details
- Sights: Cool-toned lighting (more blue below the waterline), condensation on exterior walls in morning, the constant movement of data displays, the visible ocean horizon from Deck 2's observation windows
- Sounds: Low mechanical hum everywhere; at night, the rhythm of the water processing is audible as a deep pulse; footsteps echo differently in the warrens
- Smells: Recycled air (slightly sweet, slightly chemical), coffee from The Hub (the real smell of normalcy), seawater when airlocks cycle, the medical bay's chemical something-underneath
- Textures: Metal grating underfoot in the warrens; smooth composite on admin decks; the slight tackiness of handrails in high-humidity corridors

### Weather influence
Above-deck sections are vulnerable to storm conditions; the station has been through three major weather events in the past decade. Storm protocols lock down Decks 1–3 and create effective communication isolation from shore. Vasquez chose his timeline around a predicted storm window.

---

## Time-based changes
- Day: Active, busy, social. The Hub crowded at mealtimes. Noise level high on Operations floor.
- Night: Skeleton crew. Long corridors feel longer. Data center access is logged less frequently reviewed. Night shift develops its own culture — looser, slightly paranoid.
- Storms: Everything changes. External comms degraded. Station self-contained. People rely on each other more because they have no choice.

---

## Associated characters
- **Maya Chen**: Deck 2 analyst; has access to Decks 2–8 standard, the data center by request
- **Tomás Devlin**: Deck 7 engineering; knows the warrens better than anyone currently posted
- **Director Vasquez**: Deck 1 (restricted); his authority extends to every deck in practice
- **Commander Park**: Nominally above Vasquez in the chain of command; physically present on Deck 2

---

## Story function
The station is both a physical trap and a social ecosystem. Maya cannot easily leave (transport is scheduled, not on demand). The people she needs to trust or avoid are all in the same building. The station's information systems are the battleground — whoever controls the data controls the narrative. The physical layout matters: the warrens give Maya routes that bypass the logged corridors; the data center is the prize; the storm window is the clock.
