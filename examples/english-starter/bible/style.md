# Style Guide — Example: Near-Future Thriller

## Narrative voice

### Point of view
- [x] Third person limited (focused on: Maya Chen, with occasional chapters from secondary POV)

### Tense
- Primary tense: past simple
- Secondary tenses: past perfect for flashbacks, present for internal thoughts (in italics)

### Narrator tone
Close and intimate — stays tight inside Maya's perspective. Observational, slightly paranoid, dry wit.
External world filtered through her professional skepticism.

---

## Language & vocabulary

### Register
Standard to slightly technical. Scientists and engineers speak with precision; corporate figures use vague euphemisms. Contrast between authentic voice and institutional doublespeak is intentional.

### Era-appropriate language
- Target era: 2047, near-future Earth
- Forbidden modern terms: current slang that would date the text ("literally", "vibe", "slaps")
- Encouraged vocabulary: Functional neologisms for new tech when first introduced (then use naturally); existing words repurposed ("skin" for biosensor layer, "ghost" for anonymous data packet)

### Expressions & interjections
- "Not your problem" (Maya's deflection habit — she says it ironically when it *is* her problem)
- "Run the numbers" (used literally and metaphorically)
- Characters rarely exclaim; they understate

---

## Sentence structure

### Average length
- Target: 12–20 words per sentence
- Maximum: 35 words (longer sentences signal character overwhelm or complex tech exposition)

### Complexity
- [x] Simple sentences preferred in action and tension
- [x] Compound sentences allowed in dialogue and reflection
- [ ] Complex nested clauses allowed (sparingly, only in exposition)

### Rhythm
Punchy during action sequences. Flowing in quiet character moments. Sentence fragments acceptable for internal monologue and shock reactions.

---

## Paragraphs & chapters

### Paragraph length
- Average: 2–4 sentences
- Maximum: 6 sentences (break up data dumps into dialogue or action beats)

### Chapter length
- Target: 2,500–4,000 words
- Structure: Scene-sequel pattern. Scene = goal → conflict → disaster. Sequel = reaction → dilemma → decision.

### Chapter endings
- [x] Cliffhangers required for action chapters
- [x] Questions/mystery hooks for investigation chapters
- [x] Soft transitions allowed only for interlude/character chapters (max 2 per story)

---

## Dialogue

### Formatting
- Quotation style: " " (standard English)
- Attribution style: after dialogue when possible; "said" preferred; action beats over dialogue tags when character is moving

### Tags
Preferred dialogue tags:
- said, asked, replied, added, cut in, muttered, called

Forbidden:
- hissed (cliché), spat, breathed, smiled (not a way to speak)

### Balance
- Dialogue to prose ratio: 35% dialogue

---

## Description

### Sensory details
Priority senses: visual > auditory > tactile
Smell only when narratively significant (blood, chemical burn, rain on hot pavement)

### Length
- Scene setting: brief (2–3 sentences establish location, 1–2 return to it during scene)
- Character description: minimal (defining feature or two, revealed through action)
- Action sequences: sparse prose, white space, short paragraphs

### Techniques
- [x] Similes encouraged (grounded in Maya's technical background)
- [ ] Metaphors encouraged (used sparingly; generic metaphors forbidden)
- [ ] Personification allowed (only when clearly intentional)
- [x] Lists/enumerations style: in-sentence, comma-separated; bullet lists only in documents Maya reads

---

## Tone & mood

### Overall tone
Tense, intelligent, propulsive. Think near-future Crichton or early Le Carré — competence under pressure, institutions failing individuals. Not dystopian doom, not hopeful utopia; ambivalent about technology.

### Emotional range
Present: fear, determination, betrayal, dark humor, loyalty. Absent: sentimentality, rage, helplessness (Maya feels these but doesn't show them).

### Humor style
Dry understatement. Never jokes in action. Occasional dark irony in observation.

---

## Forbidden elements

### Content
- No torture scenes (referenced, not shown)
- No sexual content
- Violence is brief and consequence-focused, not lingered over

### Style
- No adverb pile-ups ("she said softly and quietly")
- No weather as mood-setter (no "it was a dark and stormy night")
- No dream sequences
- No omniscient narrator intrusions ("Little did she know...")
- No chapter epigraphs unless they are in-world documents
