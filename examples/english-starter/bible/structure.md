# Story Structure — Example: Near-Future Thriller

## Typical narrative arc

### Act 1 — Setup (chapters 1–3)
- Establish Maya's world: her job at Station Alpha, her careful invisibility
- Introduce the anomaly she's not supposed to notice
- Inciting incident: she notices it anyway and can't unknow it

### Act 2A — Rising action (chapters 4–9)
- Investigation deepens: each answer generates two more questions
- Allies appear and their trustworthiness is ambiguous
- First attempt to report through official channels fails (institutional resistance)
- Personal stakes established: someone she cares about is now in danger

### Act 2B — Complications (chapters 10–14)
- Major setback: her investigation is discovered; she becomes a target
- True scope of the conspiracy revealed — larger than she imagined
- Apparent ally turns out to be compromised
- She is stripped of resources; must improvise with minimal support

### Act 3 — Climax & Resolution (chapters 15–18)
- She can't fight the institution directly; must use its own mechanisms against it
- Confrontation at the source of the cover-up
- Resolution exposes the truth — but victory is partial; the institution survives, compromised
- Epilogue: Maya's new equilibrium (changed but functional)

---

## Chapter patterns

### Typical chapter structure
1. Opening hook: drop into a moment of action or decision (no scene-setting opening paragraphs)
2. Scene development: goal pursued, obstacle encountered
3. Complication or discovery: something changes the situation
4. Ending hook: new question, raised stakes, or abrupt cut

### Recurring elements
- [x] Maya runs an analysis — it contradicts what she was told
- [x] Someone warns her off, which confirms she's on the right track
- [x] Technology solves one problem and creates another
- [x] Competent professionals face systemic obstruction, not incompetence

---

## Mystery mechanics

### Clue distribution
- Early clues: available in plain sight, misread as routine (manifest anomaly, routine maintenance log)
- Mid clues: require cross-referencing separate data sources
- Late clues: the physical evidence everyone thought was destroyed

### Red herrings
One false lead is planted by the antagonist deliberately. One is a genuine coincidence Maya over-interprets. Both are resolved before the climax — she must correct her own assumptions.

### Resolution pattern
Maya cannot expose the conspiracy alone. She uses the institution's own audit mechanisms, forcing an internal review that cannot be buried. Resolution is procedural, not action-hero.

---

## Tension management

### Build-up techniques
- Information asymmetry: reader knows slightly more than Maya (or less — varies by chapter)
- Time pressure introduced at Act 2B midpoint (30-hour deadline)
- Isolation: Maya's support network is reduced each act

### Release valves
- Brief chapter showing Maya's life outside the investigation (used once, Act 2A)
- Dark humor exchanges with Devlin (her reluctant ally)
- Professional wins that feel good even as the larger picture worsens
