# The Manifest Anomaly

## Logline
A data analyst on an isolated offshore station discovers a systematic cover-up in the cargo manifests — and realizes the people she'd need to report it to are the ones who created it.

## Setup
Maya Chen is eleven months into a two-year contract at Station Alpha, a deep-water processing facility in contested international waters. She is careful, competent, and deliberately unremarkable. Three weeks before a scheduled shipment, a routine weight discrepancy in a cargo manifest catches her eye — a 40% deviation from declared tonnage across multiple line items. The compounds involved have a very short list of legitimate uses. She files a standard anomaly report and expects nothing.

## Conflict
The anomaly report disappears from the system overnight. When Maya queries it, she's told the figures were corrected — but the "correction" doesn't match any data revision log she can find. Director Vasquez, who has always been pleasant to her, calls her in for a brief meeting to "clarify some confusion." He is charming and specific in a way that tells her he knows exactly which questions she asked. She realizes the report was intercepted before it reached the external audit system it was supposed to trigger.

## Complications
1. Maya begins a secondary investigation using systems that should be below anyone's notice — maintenance logs, environmental monitoring, weight sensor histories. The data builds a picture of systematic diversion of restricted industrial compounds, ongoing for at least 18 months.
2. She identifies an ally: Tomás Devlin, a floor engineer who's noticed the same discrepancies from the physical side and been told to ignore them. Together they're more capable — and more exposed.
3. The storm window closes in: a major weather event will isolate the station for 72 hours, during which Vasquez plans to run the largest transfer yet. After the storm, the compounds will be off-station and the evidence will be sanitized.

## Climax
Maya cannot expose the conspiracy through official channels — those channels are compromised. Instead, she uses the station's external audit system, which is legally required to be direct-to-regulator and tamper-evident. To get the data there, she needs 20 minutes in the Deck 11 data center during the storm, when logging is lighter — but Vasquez has had the same idea and will be running his own operation at the same time.

## Resolution
The exposure is partial and procedural: a regulatory flag that cannot be retracted. Vasquez is suspended pending investigation; he will likely escape serious consequence. The compounds are intercepted. The cover-up is documented but not yet punished.

Maya finishes her contract. She does not take another station posting. She does not know yet whether she did enough — but she knows she didn't look away.

## Themes
- Institutional complicity: how systems protect themselves from accountability
- The cost of competence: being good at your job means you notice things you'd rather not
- Moral clarity under pressure: the difference between what's legal and what's right
- Isolation as both weapon and tool
