# Designing Lore — Deep Memory Layer

Build the deep-memory layer of a setting: mythologies, legendary figures,
historical eras, relics, and sacred artifacts. This operates at the macro /
historical scale — the single-object folk belief generator is the
`folklore-generator` skill; they're complementary.

## Process

1. **Mythic origin** — pick an origin shape: sacrifice-genesis, fragmented god,
   memory-echo, recursive loop, alien catalyst. Origin myths encode what the
   culture believes about itself, not literal history.
2. **Legendary figures** — for each: archetype, the symbolic anchor they embody,
   multi-region variants (the same figure remembered differently in different
   places), and their current echoes (how they're invoked now).
3. **Historical eras** — name, defining event, *what survived but was twisted*
   (memory distorts), and how that history still presses on the present.
4. **Relics & artifacts** — type, function, origin, the cultural reactions they
   provoke, the rituals around them, and their conflict potential.
5. **Cross-reference** — link myths to biomes, civilizations, and factions; mark
   productive contradictions (variant accounts that don't reconcile are realistic,
   not errors).

## Principle

The past shapes the present (at least three generations of depth — see
`magic-systems.md`). Legends should mean something; if a myth, relic, or figure
illuminates nothing about the world or the characters, it's decoration.

## Out of scope

Doesn't design physical biomes (see `designing-worlds.md`). Doesn't write final
bible files (hand to **bible-builder**).
