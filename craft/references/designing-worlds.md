# Designing Worlds — Physical Substrate

Build the physical foundations of a setting: biomes, planetary systems, climate,
and ecological zones, with narrative tension baked in. Used by the
`civilization-builder` skill (Step 1) and any time you're creating a world,
planet, biome, or ecosystem. This is design methodology — the finished canon is
written into `bible/universe/` by **bible-builder**, never directly.

## Process

1. **Biome architecture** — for each biome: base type, an *anomaly layer* (the
   one thing that makes it not-generic), inhabitant pattern, resource signature.
2. **Biome relationships** — how biomes connect: trade, migration, conflict,
   physical adjacency. A world is its connections, not a list of zones.
3. **Planetary mechanics** — orbital pattern, rotation and time structure,
   climate zones. Derive consequences (tidal locking, seasons, day length) rather
   than asserting them.
4. **Weather as narrative** — weather must *do* something: create or block
   travel, trigger rituals, alter ecology, or affect technology. If it's just
   scenery, cut it.
5. **Contradiction injection** — plant exactly one productive paradox per biome
   and mark it GENERATIVE (a tension the story can mine, not an error to fix).

## Principle

Serve the story, imply more than you show. A world lives in its details,
assumptions, and silences — what it takes for granted is where its meaning lies.
Don't catalogue invented nouns; build pressures characters must live inside.

## Out of scope

Doesn't design cultures/societies (use the `civilization-builder` skill /
`deriving-social-systems`). Doesn't design myth and history (see
`designing-lore.md`). Doesn't write final bible files (hand to **bible-builder**).
