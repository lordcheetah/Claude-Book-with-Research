# Story Structure — Seed Vault (Verdant Earth Book 2)

*Adapted from Book 1's structure guide. The 4-act procedural-mystery shape and chapter-pattern philosophy are series-level structural DNA — proven in Book 1 and reusable. The actual plot beats below are placeholders and need full replacement once a story is chosen via story-ideator.*

## Narrative arc (placeholder shape — series-proven 4-act procedural mystery)

### Act 1 — Setup
- Establish Maialèn's world — her circuit work for the Atlantic Seed Trust, her relationship to "the record," Donostia and the Bay of Biscay terraces as home base
- The anomaly surfaces: a seed-lot viability number that traveled eleven months and two testing stations without moving — "too tidy to be true" — pointing toward Q'asa Wasi, the high-Andes cooperative seed station where the lot was received
- Inciting incident: Maialèn travels to Q'asa Wasi to audit the discrepancy in person, and her first attempt to trace it gets deflected, reframed, or absorbed into "that's just how the high-altitude routes work"

### Act 2A — Investigation
- The protagonist (and likely allies — possibly including a returning Book 1 figure in a supporting role) digs into the pattern
- The failure isn't random — it's structural
- Stakes escalate on a ticking clock specific to this story's domain (Book 1 used the algal-bloom irreversibility window; this book needs its own equivalent — a seed-viability deadline, a planting-season cutoff, a certification expiry)

### Act 2B — Complication
- The protagonist is institutionally constrained — suspended, frozen out, or placed in a conflict-of-interest position (mirrors Book 1's structural beat: the investigator becomes a suspect/liability in the eyes of the institution they're investigating)
- They continue from outside or around the formal system
- The institution behind the irregularity comes into focus — not a villain, an incentive structure making a calculated bet

### Act 3 — Resolution
- The protagonist uses some form of transparency/open-publication mechanism to force the institution to act in public (Book 1 used the Open Knowledge Compact — series canon now treats this as a proven, citable precedent; Book 2 may reuse it, reference it, or need its own analogous mechanism)
- The matter is resolved publicly and procedurally — not through confrontation
- The win is partial and honest about that; the underlying incentive structure isn't destroyed, just this particular play fails
- Land on the protagonist's own "what they learned, what's still broken" beat

## Chapter patterns

*Carried forward from Book 1 — proven series-level chapter DNA, reusable as-is:*

### Typical structure
1. Opening in a specific physical location (protagonist always grounded in place)
2. Technical discovery or investigation advance
3. Human complication (obstruction, alliance, or new information from a person)
4. Ending on an escalating question or a door closing

### Recurring elements
- [ ] Protagonist interacts with a living/working system before making a decision (Book 1's "Yara always grounded in place" — needs the new protagonist's domain-equivalent)
- [ ] The setting itself is described once per chapter — its beauty is load-bearing, the thing actually at stake made visible
- [ ] A secondary character supplies institutional/political context the protagonist lacks (Del's role in Book 1 — open whether this is a new character or a returning one)

## Tension management

### Build-up
- A domain-specific ticking clock from early in Act 1 onward (the equivalent of Book 1's bloom-irreversibility window)
- Institutional obstruction escalates chapter to chapter
- Personal stakes for the protagonist (career, standing, a relationship) rise in step with the investigation

### Release valves
- At least one scene per act showing the world working beautifully — what's actually at stake, made visible and felt
- A secondary character whose humor or warmth deflates the heaviest institutional moments
