# Style Guide — Seed Vault (Verdant Earth Book 2)

*Adapted from Book 1's style guide. Series-level DNA (voice register, era vocabulary, genre contracts, forbidden elements) is carried forward as-is — it's load-bearing across the whole series. Protagonist and setting are now locked in (see `bible/characters/maialen-etxeberria.md` and `bible/universe/qasa-wasi.md`); remaining open items are listed at the bottom.*

## Narrative voice

### Point of view
- [x] Third person limited (focused on: **Maialèn Etxeberria**)
- Carried forward from Book 1: close third, never head-hopping, never an omniscient or ironic remove from the protagonist's own competence and care.

### Tense
- Primary tense: past simple (series-consistent)
- Secondary tenses: past perfect for flashbacks; present for Maialèn's direct internal observations (in italics) — same device as Book 1, recalibrated to her own cadence: where Yara's present-tense asides were still and analytical, Maialèn's should feel more like her spoken "let me say it out loud" tic turned inward — thinking *through* a sequence rather than simply noting a fact

### Narrator tone
Close third — inside Maialèn's head, but hers is a noisier, warmer head than Yara's. Where Book 1's narration mirrored Yara's stillness (observational, restrained, dry), this book's narration should mirror Maialèn's verbal habit of thinking by enumeration and self-translation: slightly more digressive, more prone to naming things in sequence, occasionally slipping a Spanish/Basque phrase into her own internal narration before catching and re-rendering it in English — never as a gimmick, always as how she actually thinks. Same sincerity and competence-as-care as the series register; same refusal of irony about the world itself. The temperamental shift is real: Yara expressed care by going quiet and precise; Maialèn expresses it by talking a problem into the open air until its shape becomes visible.

## Language & vocabulary

### Register
Standard. Technical vocabulary used accurately and without apology — Maialèn is an expert in chain-of-custody science, seed-lot viability testing, and cross-bioregional audit work, and the reader is trusted to follow. New terms introduced in context, not defined in asides. (Series-consistent — carried forward unchanged.)

### Era-appropriate language
- Target era: **2094** (3 years after Book 1's events)
- Ecological Covenant vocabulary used naturally: "Restoration Credits," "bioregional," "the Accord," "Covenant-certified" — same vocabulary bank as Book 1, now with three more years of currency
- Forbidden: anachronistic negativity about ecological technology (people in 2094 don't find clean energy unusual or idealistic — if anything, less so than in 2091)
- Encouraged: technical precision in seed-lot viability science, chain-of-custody/certification systems, cross-bioregional cold-chain logistics, and high-altitude cooperative agriculture (Q'asa Wasi) — the equivalent load-bearing technical texture to Book 1's biofilter/water chemistry detail

### Expressions
- Contemporary professional register, not futuristic slang (series-consistent)
- Maialèn's internal voice carries Spanish/Basque cadences — self-translating mid-thought ("Demasiado limpio — too clean to be the truth"), counting and naming things aloud as a thinking technique. This is the series-equivalent of West African English cadence shaping Yara's voice: never dialect-as-spectacle, always rhythm and habit of mind
- At Q'asa Wasi, Quechua and Spanish mix in the daily speech of the people Maialèn works with — she is fluent enough in Spanish to follow easily, and the text should let that texture register without translation-as-exposition
- "The Before Time" remains available as a period reference, used sparingly

## Sentence structure

*Carried forward from Book 1 — series-level prose contract, reusable as-is:*
- Average length: 14–22 words; maximum 38
- Simple sentences in action/tension; compound in dialogue and technical explanation; nested clauses allowed in the protagonist's analytical observations
- Rhythm: precise and varied — technical passages shorter and declarative, character moments longer and more recursive, scene transitions crisp

---

## Paragraphs & chapters

- Paragraph length: average 3–4 sentences, max 6 (series-consistent)
- Chapter length: target 3,000–4,500 words — Book 1 ran 6 chapters / ~24,600 words; Book 2's chapter count is TBD pending the plan but should land in a comparable novella range unless the story clearly needs more room
- Chapter endings: cliffhangers for action/discovery chapters, open questions for investigation chapters, soft landing reserved for the final chapter (series-consistent)

---

## Dialogue

*Carried forward from Book 1 — series-level conventions:*
- Quotation style: " " (standard English)
- Attribution after dialogue when possible; action beats over tags when characters move
- Preferred tags: said, asked, replied, added, noted, cut in, continued. Forbidden: hissed, spat, breathed (as speech tags)
- Dialogue-to-prose ratio: ~30% baseline (Book 1 actually ran richer — 20–39% by chapter, averaging ~30%) — this is an investigation-driven series; internal analysis carries real weight, but conversation is how institutions become legible

## Description

*Carried forward from Book 1, recalibrated for the new setting(s):*
- Sensory priority: New Accra ran on a "green-mineral-wet" signature; this book has TWO contrasting registers to use deliberately — Donostia/Bay of Biscay (coastal, maritime, terrace-agricultural) as Maialèn's home-key, and Q'asa Wasi (thin cold air, stone, altitude light, dry-papery vault smell, the sharp warm-green contrast stepping into the nursery terraces) as the story's primary "away" setting. Let the difference between these two registers be felt, not stated.
- Scene setting: moderate, 3–5 sentences; character description minimal and functional; technical systems detailed when central to plot, brief otherwise
- Similes grounded in Maialèn's own expertise — chain-of-custody records, viability curves, the geometry she "sketches in the air" when thinking. (The series' signature description technique: Yara's engineering lens shaped Book 1's imagery; Maialèn's audit-and-record lens should shape this book's the same way — "a clean record is the closest thing to an honest witness" is both her belief and a usable descriptive frame)

## Tone & mood

### Overall tone
Series-consistent: purposeful, investigative tension set against a backdrop of genuine beauty and a world that works. The threat is to something worth protecting — not a backdrop of decay.

### Emotional range
Determination, frustration, wonder, betrayal, satisfaction. No despair — solarpunk doesn't go there. (Series-level hard constraint, carried forward unchanged.)

### Humor
Dry, professional, observational — the series' humor comes from competent people's sardonic relationship with bureaucratic friction, not from wit for its own sake.

---

## Forbidden elements

*Series-level hard constraints — carried forward unchanged, non-negotiable:*
- No gratuitous violence (thriller, not action)
- No cynicism about ecological restoration or cooperative governance
- No info-dumping; no characters explaining the Covenant to each other; no modern-day-dystopian framing
- This world is genuinely better, not secretly terrible — the Collapse happened and is remembered, but the Covenant's gains are real and load-bearing

## Genre conventions

*Series DNA — identical contract to Book 1, the thing that makes this a "Verdant Earth" book and not just "a thriller set in the same universe":*

### Solarpunk
- The world is genuinely better — characters live inside the Covenant without explaining it
- Solutions are collective and procedural, not individual heroism — wins are partial and honest about that
- Labor and cooperation are the engine of the world; technology is integrated with ecology, not opposed to it
- Optimism is load-bearing, not naive

### Procedural thriller
- Investigative logic airtight and fair-play — every clue discoverable by the reader from available evidence
- Each chapter advances the investigation AND raises a new obstacle or question
- **The antagonist is an institution, not a person** — series-defining rule, carried forward exactly. Whatever "irregularity in the seed bank network" drives this plot, it traces back to an incentive structure, not a mustache-twirling villain
- Technical accuracy matters — whatever domain this book centers (seed banking / agricultural genetics / biodiversity logistics) must behave as a real system would

### What this is NOT
- Not a story where the protagonist is exceptional or uniquely heroic — they win by doing their job correctly
- Not dystopian — institutional frustration is not institutional condemnation
- Not a heist or action story — no physical confrontation, no chase sequences
