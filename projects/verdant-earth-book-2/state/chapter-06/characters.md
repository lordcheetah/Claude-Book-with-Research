# Character States at End of Chapter 6 (End of Book)

## Maialèn Etxeberria

- **Location:** Donostia office, Atlantic Seed Trust — three weeks after returning from Q'asa Wasi (book's final scene). Earlier in the chapter: Watching Wall (dawn), records room (mid-morning and late afternoon), Long Terraces (midday), Watching Wall (evening).
- **Emotional state:** Moves through the chapter from relief (the Tribunal's acceptance and the Subpanel's administrative hold — "the clock didn't run out, it stopped") → companionable and grounded (planting day, the foot-plow lesson, working alongside Doña Encarnación and Rosalía) → grief-without-despair (the two-valleys-over cooperative's lost heritage line — "that's the difference between a loss and an ending") → quiet resolve (the audit report's three findings and signed open question; the closing margin note in Donostia). Ends the book settled — not triumphant, not anxious, simply at rest with an outcome that is real but partial.
- **Enumeration habit — two uses this chapter:**
  1. [Day 12, dawn, Watching Wall] A completed three-item count — petition accepted, case officer pending; Subpanel session on hold, no new date; citation count climbing across three bioregions, independent of the Tribunal. The first time in the book the count finishes with nothing missing and nothing in it frightening her: "I think that's just what it feels like when the count finishes and everything in it is — fine. Not solved. Fine."
  2. [Day 12, midday, Long Terraces] A lighter, five-item "partial win" recap mid-planting, including the two-valleys-over lot as item four — explicitly naming the difference between Q'asa Wasi's recoverable 13-point loss and another cooperative's actual ending. Closes with: "That is what a partial win looks like."
- **Air-sketching gesture:** Remains closed/unused this chapter — no new development. (Last active use was the negative callback in Chapter 5's germination trial; this chapter doesn't revisit it.)
- **AST-7734 (registration status):** Began the chapter with the Trust's message that her registration is "under review pending consultation with the Compact's standards office" — a new, open-ended institutional question attached to her name for the first time in eighteen years. She wrote herself a note: *"AST-7734: under review. Filed anyway. Would file again."* Three weeks later, the status has updated to "under continued review, no further action required at this time" — not closed, but quieter. She takes this, for now, as "nothing, yet."
- **Final state (end of book):** Back in Donostia, three weeks on. Her interim report to the Trust was filed on schedule and received without comment. She rereads the original Lot 441 transfer record — the four identical 91.00% readings that opened the book — and writes in the margin she'd left blank five weeks ago: *"Whose claim? Mine now too. I signed it forward. — M.E.G."* She closes the notebook and the satchel, and drinks her coffee while it's still warm.
- **Arc resolution:** Per her character bible's thematic statement, this arc is now fulfilled — she has moved from a question about whose claim a number represents (asked of others, of a system, of an absent author) to an understanding that a claim is also a personal, signed, answerable act, and that she is now one of the people whose signature carries that weight. The win is explicitly partial — institutional outcomes remain open — but her personal resolution is complete and stable.
- **Key relationships at book's end:**
  - Bixente — fully steady; present at both bookend scenes (Watching Wall dawn, Donostia close). His line "Some files don't really close... that's its own kind of finished" is the chapter's closest thing to a thesis statement, delivered lightly.
  - Inés — companionable partnership at its most relaxed this chapter (the records-room afternoon: tea, unhurried, "two people building something against a clock" replaced by simple shared presence). The grandmother's-ledger story at the Watching Wall is the emotional high point of their relationship in the book — an act of trust and continuity passed from Inés to Maialèn.
  - Tomás — thread closed via a relayed message (through Inés): glad to have been wrong about the timing, not the principle. Maialèn's reply ("glad he said it plainly... that's not nothing either") closes the loop with mutual respect intact.
  - Doña Encarnación — the "two knuckles" correction is a small, wordless act of acceptance/permission — Maialèn is now someone worth correcting properly, not just tolerated.
  - Rosalía — brief, warm, consistent (foot-plow banter, "the first real laugh in days").

## Inés Huanca Quispe (Seed Council Chair, Q'asa Wasi)

- **Location:** Records room (Day 12, dawn handoff and late afternoon) → Long Terraces (midday) → Watching Wall (evening).
- **Emotional state:** Steady and warm throughout — the dynamic with Maialèn this chapter is fully companionable, no longer task-driven. She delivers the two-valleys-over news with the same unsentimental accuracy she's shown all book ("not enough to replant a full terrace... that's the difference between a loss and an ending, and it isn't a difference either of us did anything to deserve"), but this chapter also shows her reflective and personal for the first time — the audit-report scene and the grandmother's-ledger story.
- **What she has done/shared this chapter:**
  - Brought Maialèn down from the Watching Wall to the records room, then the Long Terraces, for planting day.
  - Shared that the rest of the redistributed backup stock is going to a cooperative two valleys over, whose heritage oca line is effectively lost (under 30% viability) and is being outcrossed to save what genetics remain.
  - Sat with Maialèn through the audit-report drafting — companionable presence, occasional commentary ("Thirteen points... written down properly, finally").
  - Offered the chapter's summary of the cooperative's institutional identity: "Keeper, not innovator... though today, for a few hours, we innovated a little."
  - Relayed Tomás's message (glad to be wrong about the timing, not the principle).
  - At the Watching Wall, told the story of her grandmother's 1991 ledger entry — written in pencil, in an unofficial notebook, during the cooperative's worst years, with no expectation of an audience: "Someone has to remember what grew here."
- **Closing reflections:** "Keeper, not innovator" — Inés's recurring self-description for the cooperative, now explicitly tied to the grandmother's-ledger story as its origin and justification. The cooperative's identity (recordkeeping as an act of care, independent of whether anyone outside ever reads it) is the emotional throughline of her arc in this book.
- **Relationship to Maialèn:** At its warmest and most equal point in the book — co-petitioners, working partners, and now something closer to friends. No unresolved tension between them.

## End-of-Book Note

This is the final chapter of Book 2. All main-cast arcs are at a stable resting point appropriate for a series close: Maialèn's personal arc (the meaning of a signed claim) is fully resolved; the institutional arcs (Tribunal review, her registration status, the Subpanel's session, the geographic scope of the methodology problem, the "New Accra" thread) are deliberately left open at varying time horizons, from "weeks" to "years" to "unconfirmed indefinitely." No character ends the book in active conflict with another; the unresolved tension is entirely between individuals and institutions, which is consistent with the book's thematic register and leaves clear, non-urgent hooks for Book 3.
