# Synopsis — Seed Vault (Verdant Earth Book 2)

## Logline
When a cross-bioregional seed-lot auditor follows a number that shouldn't be stable to a high-Andes cooperative, she uncovers a twenty-year-old scoring methodology that has been systematically erasing the network's most irreplaceable varietal lines — not through sabotage, but through the quiet institutional power of deciding what gets measured and how.

---

## Setup
It is 2094. Maialèn Etxeberria, a seed-lot audit specialist for the Atlantic Seed Trust, is closing a routine transfer reconciliation when she finds it: a viability certificate for Lot 441, a high-altitude tuber varietal that left her vault in Donostia eleven months ago and arrived at Q'asa Wasi — a cooperative seed station in the Peruvian highlands at 3,850 meters — registered at exactly 91% viability. Same number, same decimal, two different testing stations, two technicians who have never met. Not even by the rounding.

She has spent eighteen years trusting that a number that travels that far without moving is a number telling a polite lie.

Maialèn travels to Q'asa Wasi to find out whose lie it is. She expects to close the assignment in two weeks.

---

## Conflict
Q'asa Wasi is not what Maialèn expected. The cooperative is meticulous — more careful, in fact, than most certified nodes on her circuit. Their seed-keepers, drawing on four centuries of unbroken continuity, maintain germination trial records that predate the Covenant itself. The lot arrived here exactly as documented. The 91% is real.

But the lot wasn't re-tested because nothing in the system flagged it as worth re-testing. Its assigned diversity-significance score — the number that determines how often a lot is monitored, funded, audited — was low enough to drop it out of the routine monitoring cycle. The number didn't move because the number was never re-checked. And the reason it was never re-checked is that someone, somewhere upstream, told the network this lot didn't matter.

Maialèn pulls on that thread. The diversity-significance scores for Q'asa Wasi's lots — and for dozens of lots across the Andean and Mediterranean circuits she knows best — are systematically wrong for certain varietal classes. Cross-referenced against Q'asa Wasi's own internal germination records (far more granular than anything in the official system), the picture is clear: the lots the official network considers low-priority are, in Q'asa Wasi's own data, performing above the baseline for their class. The scoring system and the living seed disagree.

The antagonist is not a person. It is the Accord's Genetic Diversity Preservation Council — specifically its Infrastructure Funding Advisory Subpanel, whose diversity-significance scoring methodology was designed in 2079 and has never been formally reviewed. Over twenty years, three compounding design failures have produced systematic bias: panels skewed toward commercially-adjacent cooperatives, funding criteria benchmarked to outcomes the algorithm can measure, and a taxonomic classification system that doesn't fit traditional high-altitude varietals cleanly. Nobody designed the bias. The Subpanel's members, if confronted, would be genuinely horrified.

The harm is real regardless. The lots classified as low-priority have been under-monitored, under-resourced, and quietly deprioritized for archive space. Some of them are starting to fail in the archives because no one was watching.

---

## Complications
**1.** Q'asa Wasi's Seed Council is not immediately cooperative. They know their germination records contradict the official scores — they have known this for years. They have also learned, from long experience, that when an outside auditor arrives with documentation proving that an outside system's assessment of them was wrong, the likeliest outcome is administrative disruption, not correction. Maialèn earns their trust slowly, not by demonstrating her competence (which they assume) but by demonstrating that she understands what their records mean and why they were kept.

**2.** The Atlantic Seed Trust advises Maialèn to file her interim report and close the assignment. The "irregularity" has been explained — monitoring gap, no active fraud, lot 441 is exactly what it says it is, just inadequately re-tested. Pulling on the methodology question is outside a routine audit's scope. Maialèn is not suspended or blocked; she is simply told, professionally, that her job here is done. She stays anyway. She is now working without her employer's endorsement, using her own Compact-certified credentials and the Seed Council's willingness to show her the older ledgers.

**3.** A regulatory update is moving through the Accord's Technical Council that would formalize the current diversity-scoring methodology into binding Covenant protocol. Once it passes, the methodology becomes law — challengeable, in principle, but practically entrenched. The window to formally object is measured in weeks. And the planting window for Q'asa Wasi's regional replanting program arrives at the same time: the lot in the archive turns out, on the cooperatives' own final germination test, to be lower-viability than its certificate claims — the monitoring gap was real.

---

## Climax
Maialèn, working with the Seed Council, files a formal biodiversity-harm challenge through the Accord's Rights Tribunal — the track that exists specifically for systematic ecological damage that falls below the threshold of a criminal act but above the threshold of acceptable institutional practice. The challenge requires documentation, not accusation: the scoring methodology applied to these varietal classes; the monitoring-frequency reduction that followed from low scores; the gap between the official data and Q'asa Wasi's own records; the projected outcome if the methodology is formally codified.

Q'asa Wasi's pre-Covenant germination ledgers — four centuries of continuous record — are formally entered into the public archive as verified data. They are the evidence base the Compact mechanism was designed for. Independent researchers in three bioregions begin pulling and cross-referencing within hours.

The challenge does what the Trust's internal channels couldn't: it triggers a hold on the regulatory update pending a methodology review, and it flags the Subpanel for a formal audit of its scoring criteria. The harm it names is specific, documented, and on the record.

---

## Resolution
The planting window arrives. The lot in the archive is, on final germination trial, running at 78% viability — a full thirteen points below its certificate. The monitoring gap was real; the lot degraded undetected. Q'asa Wasi's nursery terraces have maintained backup propagation stock for this varietal off-network, in exactly the kind of parallel unofficial practice the official system doesn't have a category for. "The seed doesn't lie about whether it grew." The regional replanting goes forward. The cooperative's unofficial backup practice is what saves the season.

Maialèn's final audit report is not clean, and it is not catastrophic. It documents what she found, in order, including what she doesn't yet know: the methodology review will take years, the Subpanel's composition will be harder to change than its criteria, and the lots that were de-prioritized over twenty years are not all recoverable. The file closes with three findings and an open question. "Three is a pattern that hasn't finished yet."

She signs it and puts it in the record.

---

## Themes
- **The record as a claim**: a certification document is not a neutral description of the world — it is an assertion made by a process, and every process embeds the assumptions and blind spots of the people who designed it
- **Institutional harm without institutional intent**: the most durable damage is often done by systems that nobody is monitoring because everyone trusts the design
- **The knowledge that predates the documentation**: Q'asa Wasi's four centuries of unbroken seed-keeping represents a form of ecological intelligence that the Covenant's data infrastructure was not built to capture — and the story is a test of whether the infrastructure can learn to recognize what it's missing
- **What it costs to stand behind your signature**: Maialèn's arc is about the difference between signing correctly and signing honestly — and about the auditor she decides to be when those two things don't align
