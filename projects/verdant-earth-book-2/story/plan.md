# Chapter Plan — Seed Vault (Verdant Earth Book 2)

*6 chapters, target ~20,000–25,000 words. Solarpunk procedural thriller, third person limited (Maialèn Etxeberria). Antagonist: the Accord's Genetic Diversity Preservation Council / its scoring methodology.*

---

## Chapter 1: Lot 441
**Function**: Setup / inciting anomaly
**Location**: Donostia, Atlantic Seed Trust northern verification office → departure
**POV focus**: Maialèn Etxeberria

**Events**:
- Open in Donostia: Maialèn at her records room, closing out a circuit of audits. Establish her world — the Basque coast, the terrace outside the window, her relationship with the work (the satchel, the enumeration habit, the faith in the record)
- The anomaly: while cross-referencing a transfer log, she finds Lot 441 — a high-altitude tuber varietal, Donostia to Q'asa Wasi, eleven months, two testing stations, 91.00% viability both ends. She talks through why this is wrong, aloud, to an empty office. "That's three reasons it should have moved. Three is a pattern."
- Bixente Agirre: brief scene establishing their working relationship and his observation that she's "found something." He cannot join her immediately — pulled to another assignment. She will be alone at Q'asa Wasi for the first stretch.
- Travel sequence: departure from Donostia, the physical transition from coastal to altitude — the Bay of Biscay receding, the high terrain arriving. The thinning air as she arrives in the Cusco region
- Q'asa Wasi: first impression — the pass road, the Watching Wall, the stone-and-slope architecture. The thin air as a physical fact. The cooperative is beautiful, functional, clearly doing exactly what it says it does
- First meeting with the Seed Council (the chair — a woman in her 60s whose family has held the lead terrace-keeping role for three generations): polite, precise, not unfriendly, but calibrated — they have hosted many auditors. Maialèn is measured and measured back
- Close: she runs the arrival number against the departure number herself. It matches. The lot is real. The record is telling the truth. She almost files a clean report. Then she asks herself: truth about what, exactly?

**Ends with**: Maialèn requesting the monitoring-cycle records for Lot 441 — specifically, the re-test schedule that should have triggered at the intermediate station.

## Chapter 2: The Monitoring Gap
**Function**: Investigation advance / first escalation failure
**Location**: Q'asa Wasi (Vault House records room; Nursery Terraces)
**POV focus**: Maialèn

**Events**:
- The monitoring records: Lot 441 was scheduled for re-test at the intermediate station but flagged as "below monitoring threshold" — its diversity-significance score (D-score) of 2.1 meant the re-test cycle was suspended automatically. This is operating as designed.
- Maialèn pulls the D-score record. 2.1 is the floor for "limited-resource monitoring." She has seen low D-scores before — but 2.1 for a high-altitude Andean tuber varietal strikes her as wrong. Not falsified. Just wrong.
- Cross-reference: she pulls D-scores for Q'asa Wasi's other outgoing lots from the last five years. Most fall below 3.5. The Atlantic-circuit average is 5.2. She pulls D-scores for Mediterranean-circuit lots in the same varietal classes: 5.1 average for comparable crops.
- Walking with the Council chair on the Nursery Terraces: Maialèn observes the morning germination check, asks careful questions. The chair answers carefully. There is a moment — brief, dense — when Maialèn mentions the D-score discrepancy and the chair's expression shifts in a way that Maialèn catalogs and does not yet interpret. The chair says the cooperative is "familiar with the scoring system."
- First escalation: Maialèn contacts the Atlantic Seed Trust, flags the monitoring-gap finding and the D-score discrepancy as worth investigating. The Trust's response: the monitoring gap is a known limitation of the current priority framework; her finding is noted; no further action recommended at this time. The language is professional and has clearly been composed by someone other than a scientist.
- Close: Maialèn goes back to the records room. She writes in her satchel notebook: "Three things. D-score floor, monitoring suspension, pattern across the circuit. Three is not a coincidence, three is a pattern that hasn't finished yet."

**Ends with**: Maialèn asking the Seed Council for their internal germination records — not the official Accord-format records, but the cooperative's own ledgers. The Council chair pauses for a long time before answering.

## Chapter 3: The Old Ledgers
**Function**: Investigation deepens / trust-building / the discrepancy becomes visible
**Location**: Q'asa Wasi (Vault House records room; the Long Terraces — working alongside the seed-keepers)
**POV focus**: Maialèn

**Events**:
- The Council chair does not immediately open the ledgers. She asks Maialèn to spend a morning on the Long Terraces first — working, not auditing. The cooperative needs to know what kind of auditor she is before they show her the older records.
- Terrace morning: Maialèn works alongside the seed-keepers. This is not the kind of verification she does, and she is clumsy at it in ways she finds clarifying. The texture of the place becomes real — what grows here, at what altitude, in what soil, and why it matters. The seed-keepers treat their varietals as having personalities. One old woman, who has maintained the same tuber line for forty years, says a sentence that lodges: "The paper says what the paper was given. I know what this plant does."
- The ledgers: the Council chair opens the locked cabinet in the records room. Maialèn spends the afternoon with pre-Covenant documentation — handwritten, meticulously organized by year and varietal, going back to the 1990s. The germination rates recorded here are consistent, precise, and in several cases significantly higher than the official D-score would predict.
- The discrepancy: for the specific varietal classes the D-score flags as "limited significance" — the high-altitude tubers and Andean grains — the cooperative's own records show performance levels that should qualify them for a D-score of 5+ under any reasonable calculation. The official scores appear to apply taxonomic classifications that don't fit these varietals well; they're being measured against a template that wasn't designed for them.
- Maialèn, at the records table, aloud: "It's — how do we say — it's not wrong, exactly. It's too tidy. Demasiado limpio. The methodology is applying the right formula to the wrong plant."
- The Council chair, watching: "We have known this for eleven years. We filed three comments during the 2083 methodology review period. They were noted and not incorporated."

**Ends with**: Maialèn, back in her room, pulling up the 2079 methodology documentation for the Genetic Diversity Preservation Council — specifically, who designed the taxonomic classification system, and when it was last reviewed. She starts reading. The chapter closes on her face in the glow of the screen, still reading, an hour past midnight.

## Chapter 4: The Subpanel
**Function**: Investigation climax — the institution identified / complication: the regulatory window
**Location**: Q'asa Wasi; remote research (communications with contacts in the network)
**POV focus**: Maialèn

**Events**:
- The methodology's design history: the 2079 scoring criteria were developed by a working group of six — three of them from cooperatives with significant commercial crop programs, one a representative from a certification-adjacent advisory body. The taxonomic classification tables were based on a dataset that was not collected in high-altitude contexts. There is no evidence anyone was trying to bias the outcome. There is also no evidence anyone subsequently checked whether it had.
- The Subpanel's current composition: four of the six current members have professional relationships with commercial-crop development cooperatives. This is on the public record. Nobody has mapped it to the score distribution.
- The regulatory update: Maialèn discovers that a formal protocol revision is moving through the Technical Council that would codify the current D-score methodology into binding Covenant standard. The comment period closed six weeks ago. A vote is scheduled in eleven weeks. Once it passes, the methodology isn't practice — it's law.
- Bixente arrives. He reads the D-score comparison Maialèn has built and immediately spots what she missed: the D-score distribution isn't just lower for Andean varietals — it's lower specifically for lots that were routed through the Q'asa Wasi circuit, compared to equivalent varietals on other circuits. The bias is geographically patterned. The algorithm isn't just misclassifying plants; it's misclassifying this cooperative's plants more than comparable ones elsewhere.
- Optional: a name surfaces from Book 1's institutional world — possibly connected to the Subpanel's history — that reminds Maialèn (and the reader) how small the certification-adjacent world is. This is a reference, not a subplot: three sentences, then the plot continues.
- The Trust's second response: Maialèn formally flags the regulatory timeline and the Subpanel composition data. The Trust responds that this is "outside the scope of the current audit assignment" and requests she file her interim report within the week.
- Close: Maialèn talks through her situation out loud to Bixente. She is not accusing anyone. She is saying that a process filled in a form correctly — for a twenty-year-old methodology applied to the wrong plant — and that form is about to become permanent.

**Ends with**: Maialèn asking the Seed Council if they are willing to formally co-file a challenge. The Watching Wall scene: Bixente stands beside her, looking down the pass road, quiet. The Council chair arrives. "We have been waiting for someone to ask that question for eleven years."

## Chapter 5: Outside the Scope
**Function**: Complication — protagonist works outside institutional sanction
**Location**: Q'asa Wasi (records room, Nursery Terraces, the Watching Wall at night)
**POV focus**: Maialèn

**Events**:
- The Trust formally advises Maialèn to close the assignment: the monitoring gap is explained, no active fraud, the methodology-review question is a policy matter outside audit jurisdiction. The communication is professionally worded and clearly final.
- Maialèn is not suspended. She is simply told the job is done. This is, in some ways, harder — she cannot argue against suspension; she can only decide what kind of auditor she is.
- Building the challenge: Maialèn, Bixente, and the Seed Council spend four days compiling the formal biodiversity-harm challenge for the Accord's Rights Tribunal. The evidence structure:
  - The scoring methodology's misclassification of the relevant varietal classes (technical section)
  - The monitoring-frequency reduction that followed from low scores (operational impact)
  - The discrepancy between official scores and Q'asa Wasi's own germination records (evidence of measurable harm)
  - The regulatory timeline (urgency: the challenge must be filed before the codification vote)
- The older ledgers: the Seed Council formally enters their pre-Covenant germination records into the public archive via the Compact mechanism. This is the evidence layer — independent researchers can now pull and verify directly. The lodging takes nineteen minutes to begin generating independent references. (This moment mirrors Book 1's cascade, but is quieter — it's a data deposit, not an emergency disclosure. The world has seen this work before; the response is less surprised, more organized.)
- The planting window arrives: Q'asa Wasi's final germination trial on the archived Lot 441 runs. Result: 78% viability — thirteen points below the certificate. The monitoring gap was real; the lot degraded undetected. The cooperative's nursery-terrace backup stock is immediately mobilized.
- Night scene: Maialèn on the Watching Wall alone, post-trial result. "That's the number I was afraid of. That's the number the record didn't have." She calls Bixente over. He sits beside her. Neither of them says anything for a while.

**Ends with**: Maialèn filing the Rights Tribunal challenge at 04:00 Q'asa Wasi time. She files it under her own Compact credentials. She attaches her name to it as the auditor of record. She is no longer acting within her employer's scope. She is acting within her own.

## Chapter 6: The Record
**Function**: Resolution / landing
**Location**: Q'asa Wasi (Nursery Terraces at dawn; Vault House; the Long Terraces during planting); brief close back in Donostia
**POV focus**: Maialèn

**Events**:
- The challenge triggers: the Accord's Rights Tribunal acknowledges receipt and begins the formal review process. The regulatory update is placed on hold pending review — announced the same morning, two hours after Maialèn's filing. The Subpanel's scoring criteria are flagged for formal audit. The Compact data deposit has, overnight, generated independent verifications from researchers in the Atlantic, Mediterranean, and Andean bioregions.
- The Trust's response: Maialèn receives a formal communication noting that she exceeded her assignment scope and that the matter is "under review." It is not a termination. It is a sentence that could become one, depending on what happens next. She reads it twice and puts it in her satchel.
- The planting: Q'asa Wasi's regional replanting program begins. The backup stock from the nursery terraces is brought down from the Long Terraces and distributed. The community plants in the old way — with the old tools, in the early-morning cold, before the sun reaches the upper terraces. Maialèn works alongside them again, for the second time. She is still clumsy at this. The old woman who tends the tuber line works next to her and says nothing, which is a form of permission.
- The win, stated plainly: the methodology will be reviewed. The codification vote has been delayed. Q'asa Wasi's records are now in the official archive. The lots that were de-prioritized over twenty years are not all recoverable — some of them are gone. The Subpanel's composition will take longer to change than its criteria. The Rights Tribunal review will take years. "That is what a partial win looks like. It looks exactly like this."
- Maialèn's final audit report: she writes it at the records table in the Vault House, in the late afternoon light, with the Council chair looking over her shoulder (not supervising — keeping her company). The report names three findings and closes with an open question about the methodology review's scope. She signs it. She puts it in the official record.
- The Watching Wall, final: Maialèn and the Council chair stand together, looking down the pass road, waiting for the supply truck. The chair tells her something about her grandmother's ledgers — the first handwritten entry, from 1991, that the chair's own grandmother made during the worst years. What it said. Why they kept making entries when it seemed like there was no one left to read them. Maialèn doesn't say anything. She writes it in her satchel notebook.
- Close: Donostia. Maialèn at the window of her verification office, the Bay of Biscay outside, her satchel open on the table. Bixente brings her coffee. She is reading Lot 441's transfer record one more time — the original, the departure number, the arrival number, the number that didn't move. "Whose claim is this?" She knows now. She writes the answer in the margin.

**Ends with**: The last line should close on her signature — and what she has learned it means to put her name on something.

## Recurring chapter elements (series DNA)
- Maialèn interacts with a living growing system before or during a key decision in every chapter (Ch1: terraces on arrival; Ch2: nursery germination check; Ch3: morning terrace work; Ch4: observing the planting trials; Ch5: backup stock mobilization; Ch6: planting day)
- Q'asa Wasi's beauty is described once per chapter — the terraces, the altitude light, the sensory signature of the place — because it is the thing actually at stake
- Bixente provides the grounding observation Maialèn cannot see from inside the records (arrives Ch4; present Ch5-6)
- Maialèn enumerates aloud at each chapter's investigative pivot: the habit is her thinking voice, and it cues the reader that something has been found

## Clue distribution
- Ch1: the number that didn't move (the "too tidy" anomaly)
- Ch2: the monitoring-threshold mechanism (low D-score → no re-test) — innocent explanation, but explains nothing about whether the score is right
- Ch3: the ledger discrepancy (cooperative's own records vs. official D-scores)
- Ch4: the methodology's design history + Subpanel composition + regulatory timeline
- Ch5: the germination trial result (78%) — the monitoring gap was real; the harm is demonstrated
- Ch6: confirmation and public record
