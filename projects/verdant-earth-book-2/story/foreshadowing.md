### The Lot 441 Transfer Record / "The Space Left Blank" — Margin Note
- **Type**: Chekhov's gun / thematic (bookend image)
- **Planted**: Chapter 01 — the original transfer record for Lot 441: four identical 91% viability readings (Donostia departure, Lisbon retest, Guayaquil retest, Q'asa Wasi arrival), with a blank margin beside the arrival figure that Maialèn does not yet know what to write in.
- **Reinforced**: Chapter 05 — the 78% germination trial result is entered into the formal challenge document and Maialèn's own audit report as Finding 3, formally documenting the thirteen-point gap the original record's blank margin had been waiting to explain.
- **Paid off**: Chapter 06, three weeks later (Donostia coda) — Maialèn rereads the original transfer record one final time, now understanding the four identical readings not as a measurement but as "the framework's own claim about itself... repeated four times until it looked, to anyone reading it cold, exactly like the truth." - **Assessment**: Earned, and the book's strongest single image. The blank margin, established in Chapter 1 as an unremarked-upon detail, is held completely untouched for five chapters and then filled in the book's final scene with a line that resolves both this plant and the "Whose claim is this?" thread (below) simultaneously. The three-week time-skip to Donostia gives the moment room to be reflective rather than triumphant — Maialèn rereads the record with full knowledge of everything that followed, and the margin note functions as both a closing of this specific document's loop and a personal statement about what her signature now means after eighteen years. This is the book's bookend image and lands as intended.

### "Whose Claim Is This?"
- **Type**: thematic / mystery (recurring internal question)
- **Planted**: Chapter 01 — Maialèn's recurring question about institutional vs. personal ownership of findings and records, first voiced (in different words) while staring at the blank margin: "Someone is telling the truth and someone is telling a polite lie, and I would like, please, to find out which is which before I decide who."
- **Reinforced**: Ch02-05 (see prior entries — the question recurs across the book's institutional-document beats).
- **Paid off**: Chapter 06, Donostia coda — resolved in two stages. First, institutionally: Maialèn realizes the 91% claim was never a person's lie or truth, but "the framework's own claim about itself" — a closed loop with no individual author, made by a system "on behalf of a framework that benefited from nothing being looked at again either." Second, personally: she answers her own original question by writing "Mine now too" in the record's margin — claiming authorship/responsibility for what she found, even though the win is partial and the claim she's making is now hers, by choice, rather than something to be traced back to someone else.
- **Assessment**: Earned. The two-stage resolution — first dissolving the question's original premise (there is no single person to blame), then replacing it with a personal answer (Maialèn claims it for herself) — gives this thread a satisfying shape that avoids both a tidy villain reveal and a hollow "no one was responsible" shrug. The closing meditation on what a signature means ("I checked this, and it is accurate" vs. "I am the one who checked, and I am answerable for what I found") gives the book's central audit/record motif its clearest thematic statement.

### Record vs. Measurement — The Difference Between a Claim and a Reading
- Appearances: Ch01-05 (see prior entries — substantially enacted by Ch05). Ch06 — reaches its final and most complete articulation: Maialèn's audit report formally separates "the system worked as designed" (Finding 1) from "the system was built wrong and nobody looked" (Finding 2) from "this had real consequences" (Finding 3) — three findings that map precisely onto the record-vs-measurement distinction this thread has tracked since Chapter 1. The Donostia coda delivers the thread's final formulation: the 91% reading was never a measurement at all but "a claim, made by an automated process... The claim hadn't really been *this lot is at ninety-one percent.* The claim had been *this lot does not need to be looked at again*."
- Building toward: COMPLETE. This thread's full arc — from "the record said one thing, the measurement said another" (early chapters) to "the record was never a measurement, it was a claim a system made about itself" (Ch06) — represents the book's central thematic throughline, and it lands in the same scene as the book's bookend image (the margin note). No further development needed; this is the book's resolved thematic core.
