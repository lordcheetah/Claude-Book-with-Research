### The Locked Cabinet
- **Type**: Chekhov's gun
- **Planted**: Chapter 01 — "a locked cabinet in the near corner that she noted and did not mention"
- **Reinforced**: —
- **Intended payoff**: The cabinet holds pre-Covenant ledgers belonging to the Seed Council; it is opened (or its contents disclosed) in Chapter 3 during the ledger disclosure scene.
- **Expected by**: Chapter 03
- **Urgency**: MEDIUM
- **Notes**: The planting is clean and deliberate — the prose marks it with exactly the kind of specific, unnamed attention that signals a Chekhov's gun. The cabinet must be addressed before the end of Act 1 or the silence becomes conspicuous.

- **Planted**: Chapter 01 — "Three. That's three reasons it should have moved... Three is not a coincidence. Three is a pattern." Maialèn counts aloud to signal she has found a pattern; the count is both her analytical method and her tell.
- **Reinforced**: —
- **Intended payoff**: Recurs when she identifies the D-score distribution (Chapters 2–3) and again at the regulatory window discovery (Chapter 4), each time serving as a structural marker that she has locked onto something real.
- **Expected by**: Chapter 02 (first recurrence to establish as habit rather than incident)
- **Urgency**: MEDIUM

### The Monitoring-Cycle Suspension
- **Type**: mystery / setup
- **Planted**: Chapter 01 — Maialèn asks to see "the re-test schedule that should have triggered at the intermediate station in Guayaquil. The one that should have caught any viability drift before the lot completed its transit." The chapter closes before the records are produced; the reason the monitoring cycle was suspended is the open question the chapter ends on.
- **Reinforced**: —
- **Intended payoff**: Chapter 2, when Maialèn learns the D-score mechanism that caused the suspension (D-scores apparently qualified the lot for reduced monitoring, suppressing the mandatory retest trigger).
- **Expected by**: Chapter 02
- **Urgency**: MEDIUM
- **Notes**: This is the chapter's closing hook; it must pay off quickly. A mystery planted on the final page with no Chapter 2 answer will feel like stalling. The payoff does not need to be complete — partial disclosure is acceptable — but Maialèn must learn *something* about why the cycle was suspended before Chapter 2 ends.

- **Reinforced**: —
- **Intended payoff**: Chapter 6, when the final planting trial takes place on the nursery terraces; the sensory signature planted here gives that scene its grounding in something the reader has already felt.
- **Expected by**: Chapter 06
- **Urgency**: LOW

Intermediate reinforcement (a passing mention of the nursery terraces in Chapters 3 or 4) would deepen the payoff without requiring additional scenes. The contrast structure — cold mineral exterior vs. warm green interior — may also be doing thematic work around concealment and vitality.

- **Reinforced**: —

- **Expected by**: Chapter 04
- **Urgency**: LOW
- **Notes**: His absence in Chapters 1–3 is itself part of the plant — the reader knows he would see something she's missing. His eventual arrival must deliver a concrete observation that justifies this setup. The "vault intuition" description in Chapter 1 is the specific capability being seeded; the payoff must be a moment where physical-spatial reading of the landscape produces the insight, not just his presence as a plot device.

## Thematic Threads

### Record vs. Measurement — The Difference Between a Claim and a Reading
- Appearances: Ch01 ("a record was not a measurement — it was a claim, made by someone, at a specific moment, for a specific reason"), Ch01 ("Not a wrong number. A number that was never rechecked.")
- Building toward: The book's central argument about institutional trust — that systems designed to verify can be subverted not through falsification but through the suppression of re-examination. The D-score mechanism ultimately works by making re-examination unnecessary by design, which is harder to prosecute than fraud.

### The Thin Air / Altitude as Honest Disclosure
- Appearances: Ch01 ("a slight widening at the edge of each breath, a reminder that she was working harder for the same amount of air"), Ch01 ("Give yourself a day before you trust your own head")
- Building toward: Altitude as a recurring figure for conditions that do not deceive you — the mountain charges an honest entry fee. The regulatory and institutional systems that fail in this story are ones that hide their costs. Altitude does not hide its cost. TBD whether this resolves as an explicit thematic statement or remains structural.

### The Watching Wall — Known and Unknown Arrivals
- Appearances: Ch01 ("three people standing along the long stone terrace-edge... watching, she realized, at the transport she had arrived on... they watched with the unhurried attention of people who had been watching this road their whole lives and knew everything that arrived along it")
- Building toward: TBD. The Watching Wall is described as natural, not formal — a place where people stand when they want to know what is coming. This may develop into a motif about who is watching whom, and whether Q'asa Wasi's awareness of arrivals extends to awareness of what has been sent to them (i.e., doctored lot records).

### Layers of Engineering — Old Technique, New Material
- Appearances: Ch01 ("sections where the original Inca-period stonework showed beneath the Covenant-era composite resurfacing, a layer of engineering on top of a layer of engineering on top of a layer of engineering, each one saying the same thing about the mountain"), Ch01 (the Vault House: "the new materials married to the old technique so naturally that you had to look carefully to see where one ended and the other began")
- Building toward: The D-score system is itself a layer placed on top of existing verification architecture. Its danger is that it looks like continuity — new technique married to old structure — while actually suppressing what the old structure was designed to do. TBD whether this surfaces as explicit dialogue or remains a structural echo.
