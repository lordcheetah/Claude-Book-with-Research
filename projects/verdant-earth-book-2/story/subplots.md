### The Lot 441 Anomaly
- **Type**: mystery/question
- **Introduced**: Chapter 01 — Maialèn notices that Lot 441 carried an identical viability reading of 91.00% across all four certification events spanning eleven months and two independent intermediate testing stations (Lisbon and Guayaquil), which is statistically impossible if the retests were actually performed.
- **Last advanced**: Chapter 02 — The mechanism is identified: the lot's D-score of 2.1 triggered Protocol 4.7(c)'s automated monitoring-suspension at Guayaquil, which was working exactly as designed. The D-score itself is traced to a miscalibrated comparison set of six lowland commercial cultivars (Kennebec, Yukon Gold, Shepody, Atlantic, Russet Burbank, Innovator) that share no ecological basis with a cold-tolerant Andean highland potato. The scope expands: Maialèn pulls all 43 Q'asa Wasi outgoing lots from the last five years and finds the same D-score pattern across all of them — a cluster of 2.0–3.5 against a circuit average of 5.2, a gap of more than two full points. The anomaly is no longer a single-lot finding; it is circuit-wide and systemic.
- **Expected resolution**: TBD (primary investigation thread; likely series-level or late-book)
- **Chapters since last advance**: 0
- **Notes**: The question has shifted from "were the retests performed?" to "why is the scoring methodology miscalibrated for this entire varietal class, across five years of lots?" The design-not-failure framing from Chapter 01 is now confirmed: the system worked. The comparison set was the problem. The two remaining open questions are: who drew the comparison set and why, and what the cooperative's own pre-Covenant records will show against the scored predictions. Connected to Inés's knowledge, the locked cabinet, the Trust's institutional response, and Maialèn's career stake.
