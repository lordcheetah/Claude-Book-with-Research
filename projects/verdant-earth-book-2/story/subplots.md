### The Lot 441 Anomaly
- **Type**: mystery/question
- **Introduced**: Chapter 01 — Maialèn notices that Lot 441, a cold-tolerant Andean potato varietal, carried an identical viability reading of 91.00% across all four certification events spanning eleven months and two independent intermediate testing stations (Lisbon and Guayaquil), which is statistically impossible if the retests were actually performed.
- **Last advanced**: Chapter 01 — Maialèn travels to Q'asa Wasi, confirms the final receipt documentation is clean, and formally requests the monitoring-cycle records for the intermediate Guayaquil retest that should have caught any viability drift.
- **Expected resolution**: TBD (primary investigation thread; likely series-level or late-book)
- **Chapters since last advance**: 0
- **Notes**: The core question is not whether the lot is fraudulent — the lot appears to be exactly what it claims to be — but whether the intermediate testing events were ever performed at all, and who made that decision and why. The chapter deliberately frames this as a procedural anomaly rather than a corruption case, leaving the motive and scale entirely open. Connected to Inés Huanca Quispe's guarded knowledge and the locked cabinet.

### Inés Huanca Quispe's Guarded Knowledge
- **Type**: mystery/question
- **Introduced**: Chapter 01 — The Council chair receives Maialèn personally (unusual for an auditor visit), displays measured patience throughout the records review, and watches Maialèn with the calibrating attention of someone who has hosted auditors before and learned to read which ones matter. Her manner shifts when Maialèn requests the monitoring-cycle records: "Yes. I can find those." — confirmation without question, which implies she already knows what those records will and won't show.
- **Last advanced**: Chapter 01 — Inés's willingness to retrieve the monitoring-cycle records is noted; she does not deflect, but she also does not explain.
- **Expected resolution**: TBD — likely unfolds across the middle of the book as Maialèn's relationship with Inés develops
- **Chapters since last advance**: 0
- **Notes**: The chapter establishes that Inés is not an antagonist in any simple sense — she is described as a person who "looks at what is in front of her," patient and direct. Her knowledge may be protective rather than conspiratorial. The question of what she knows and when she decided to know it is distinct from the question of what the records contain. Connected to the Lot 441 anomaly and the locked cabinet.

### The Locked Cabinet
- **Type**: dangling hook
- **Introduced**: Chapter 01 — In the records room, Maialèn observes "a locked cabinet in the near corner that she noted and did not mention." It is distinguished from the standard Trust-format archive shelving, implying it holds something outside the official documentation system; most likely the cooperative's older, pre-Covenant records.
- **Last advanced**: Chapter 01 — Introduced and deliberately set aside; Maialèn's restraint in not mentioning it is itself a characterization beat.
- **Expected resolution**: TBD — likely mid-book, once Maialèn has established enough trust or warrant to ask about it
- **Chapters since last advance**: 0
- **Notes**: The locked cabinet's contents are entirely open. The most probable reading is pre-Covenant records that predate the Trust's documentation system, which would be relevant if the anomaly in Lot 441 has a longer history. The fact that Maialèn notices it and stays silent is load-bearing: she is being careful, not oblivious. Connected to Inés's knowledge and the Lot 441 anomaly.

### Bixente's Absence
- **Type**: subplot
- **Introduced**: Chapter 01 — Bixente Agirre, Maialèn's eight-year colleague and cold-storage technician partner, has been pulled to the southern circuit (Catalonian seed bank network audit backlog) the week after the anomaly is discovered. He is unhappy about the timing, shown by saying nothing about it. Maialèn goes to Q'asa Wasi alone.
- **Last advanced**: Chapter 01 — The nature of the working partnership is established (she tells him what she finds before she tells the Trust; he "notices what she can't see"); his absence is framed as logistical but the timing creates narrative exposure.
- **Expected resolution**: TBD — Bixente will rejoin at some point; his "notices what she can't see" role suggests a beat where his perspective catches something Maialèn has been too close to
- **Chapters since last advance**: 0
- **Notes**: The chapter is careful not to make this feel ominous — Bixente's absence is bureaucratic and mundane, not suspicious. The tension is that Maialèn is now working an anomaly that is escalating past "routine audit" without her most trusted reader of physical evidence. The "keep me posted" arrangement (she tells him before the Trust) also sets up a future pressure point when the finding becomes serious enough that the order of notification matters.

### Maialèn's Career Stake
- **Type**: character arc beat
- **Introduced**: Chapter 01 — Maialèn is framed as an experienced, methodical, trusted auditor (eighteen years, her own certification numbers, her working relationship with Bixente as a baseline of professional competence). She came to Q'asa Wasi on what should be a routine reconciliation. The anomaly she has started pulling on is not routine, and the chapter ends with her opening her satchel notebook to a clean page — committed, but not yet knowing what she is committed to.
- **Last advanced**: Chapter 01 — The chapter establishes the gap between what she was sent to do (close a quarterly reconciliation) and what she has found (a systemic procedural failure that may or may not be intentional). Her professional instinct is fully engaged; her institutional standing has not yet been put at risk.
- **Expected resolution**: TBD — the arc beat will resolve when she has to make a choice between what her findings require and what her position as Trust auditor allows her to report
- **Chapters since last advance**: 0
- **Notes**: This is an internal arc, not an external subplot — the question is not what she finds but what she does with it. The chapter establishes her as someone who keeps two logs (the official one and the satchel notebook) and who has a long-standing informal arrangement with Bixente to report to him before the Trust. Both details are seeds of a later pressure point. Connected to Bixente's absence (he is her off-the-record sounding board) and to the scale of what the Lot 441 anomaly turns out to be.
