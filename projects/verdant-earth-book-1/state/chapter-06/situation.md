## Last chapter summary
Hour ~92, 5 AM, Day 4. The armed deposit fires exactly on schedule — quietly, without ceremony, a status change on a screen. Within nineteen minutes the access log starts ticking: independent researchers and cooperatives across the network begin pulling and verifying the record on their own, unprompted — the deposit is "out, real, seen," and can no longer be controlled or rotated away from. Del calls: the Technical Council has logged an automated notification against the public deposit (a mechanism noticing on its own, which "can't pretend it didn't"), and his secondment has been quietly restored overnight by someone trying to get ahead of the unfolding situation. The deposit firing has not moved the bloom one hour — Yara gathers her field kit and the mechanical override key (which answers to no credential) and goes to the eastern inlet to do, by hand, what the suppressed firmware should have been doing for months. Through the morning she walks the manual suppression sequence station by station — slow, careful, paced to what the living colonies can bear — and the cyano curve begins to bend back under her hands. At midday, Del finds her at Station Seven (sequence walked through Nine, curve bending) and delivers the institutional cascade in full: Altera's certification contracts frozen citywide pending investigation; the certification review opened formally, with Strand's signature now part of the record under examination ("not our accusation — their evidence now"); Rational Innovation issued a fast, lawyered public denial (investigation continues regardless); and Director Nwosu resigned this morning, before the review could reach her, in a manner that fits the shape of Yara's private inference (a misguided judgment call she could no longer stand behind) without ever becoming confession or proof. The Council's emergency response team arrives not long after midday, finds the manual sequence already holding the curve off its climb, and the lead states plainly: they were "inside the window" only because Yara put them there — without her, this would have been "a remediation that doesn't work." She and Del walk back toward the Upper Terraces in early afternoon; he lays out what comes next (a months-long formal review, "the same machine" that nearly buried them now serving to save them) and explicitly hands off the two threads that don't belong to two people on a bench — the Altera-board director's identity, now the Council investigation's to pursue with subpoena power neither of them has; and his own quiet, unspecified closing of an account connected to his Technist-era familiarity with Rational Innovation's playbook ("you earned letting it" / "don't tell anyone"). He peels off toward the Authority to begin his reinstatement paperwork. Late in the day, alone, Yara returns to Station Seven, runs the sensor check exactly as she did in the book's first chapter, finds the smell and the numbers finally agreeing — "the lie gone out from between them" — and writes the plain, true, boring log entry that the whole book has been about being able to write: a system running the way a system should. She walks home in the late light. Book 1 ends here.

## Orchestrator notes — END OF BOOK 1

This is the close of Book 1. Chapter 6 resolved the book's two central tensions in parallel and on the page: the chemical bloom-clock crisis (contained narrowly, by hand, with margin to spare) and the institutional-reckoning crisis (the deposit produced exactly the cascading, unstoppable, "cannot be un-seen" response the Compact promised, ahead of the clock). Both resolutions land through Yara's own competence and discipline rather than through external rescue — the Council's team arrives only to confirm that she had already done the thing that mattered.

- An unconfirmed identity behind the Altera-board / Rational Innovation connection — the cleanest seed for a sequel's inciting question
- Yara restored to (or in process of restoration to) Authority standing, now carrying public visibility and the personal cost of having forced an institution to look at itself — a different starting position than Book 1's "locked out and disbelieved"
- Del fully reinstated, his Technist-era account "closed... in the ordinary afternoon" — available as a settled, trusted partner rather than a question mark
- A city that does not yet know what nearly happened to it ("it still believes in itself") — a possible thematic or plot seed about what changes (or doesn't) once it does
- The Open Knowledge Compact mechanism, now publicly demonstrated to work — likely to have follow-on institutional consequences (reform, backlash, or both) that a sequel could explore
