## Chapter sequence

### Chapter 6: The Open Record — Day 4 (dawn through evening) — END OF BOOK 1

1. **5:00 AM, Hour ~92** — The armed deposit fires exactly on schedule. No drama on the screen — a status change, a confirmation hash, the line *"deposited to public record."* Yara feels none of the proportional weight she braced for; the act is complete and irreversible, and now two clocks run side by side: the bloom's (unmoved) and the Compact's (just begun, unstoppable).
2. **5:19 AM, Hour ~92.3** — The deposit's access log ticks from one (Yara's own deposit) to two, then three, then more — independent researchers, monitoring cooperatives, and university-affiliated readers across the network begin pulling and verifying the record on their own initiative, unprompted, within minutes of dawn breaking over their own time zones. Not a flood — a string of separate, real, independent eyes arriving at the same conclusion Yara reached alone in the reeds three days ago. The record is now permanently "out, real, seen" — beyond anyone's power to recall, rotate away from, or shut a door on.
3. **Shortly after sunrise, ~Hour 93** — Del calls: the Technical Council has logged an automated notification against the public deposit (a mechanism that "noticed on its own" and therefore "can't pretend it didn't"); his own secondment was quietly restored overnight, with no message or ceremony — "somebody upstairs... trying to get ahead of this." He confirms there's no public statement yet from Altera, Rational Innovation, or the Council, but "the machinery's turning." Promises hard news by midday. Does not mention Strand by name — leaves the thread exactly where they set it.
4. **Early morning, ~Hour 93–94** — Yara recognizes that the deposit firing has not moved the bloom one hour; she gathers her field kit and the mechanical override key — "mechanical and stupid and answers to no credential at all" — and walks down through the city's ordinary, unaware morning toward the eastern inlet. The override needs competence, not permission; the suspension took the credential but never reached the competence underneath it.
5. **Mid-morning through midday, ~Hour 94–99** — At the eastern inlet, Yara walks the manual suppression sequence by hand, station by station (Stations Seven through Nine): isolating the masked range, forcing the suppression dose the firmware had been blocking, holding, reading the manual gauges, waiting for the colonies to respond — paced exactly as slow as the living system can bear, because pushing too hard would kill the very organisms she's trying to free. At Station Eight, the manual cyano count comes down "a hair off the climb, then a hair more" — the curve she watched bend the wrong way in her kitchen finally beginning to bend back, under her own hands, with no one watching.
6. **Midday, ~Hour 99–100** — Del finds her at Station Seven, sequence walked through Nine, curve bending, washing inlet mud from her gloves. He delivers the full institutional cascade in one unbroken report, "because you'll want it once and complete and not in pieces": (a) Altera's certification contracts frozen citywide pending investigation; (b) the certification review opened formally an hour earlier, with Strand's signature now inside the record under examination — "not our accusation. Their evidence now"; (c) Rational Innovation issued a fast, lawyered public denial, with the investigation continuing regardless; and (d) **Director Nwosu resigned this morning**, stepping down before the review could reach her, framed internally as protecting "the process and the city's name" rather than as confession to sabotage. Yara receives the news of Nwosu's departure as private grief rather than vindication, and gives her the only eulogy she will get: *"She read a log faster than anyone I worked under."*
7. **Early afternoon, ~Hour 100** — The Council's emergency response team (six responders, powered equipment) arrives at the inlet "late and well — the most the clock had allowed," and finds the manual gauges already set, the override already keyed, and the curve already off the climb. The team lead reads Yara's manual sequence, confirms it is holding, and states plainly and professionally — without ceremony, without calling Yara a hero — that they are "inside the window... because somebody put us inside it before we got here," and that without this morning's manual intervention, "we'd be standing in a remediation that doesn't work."
8. **Early-to-mid afternoon, ~Hour 101–102** — Yara and Del walk the wetland path back toward the Upper Terraces. Del lays out what comes next: a months-long formal review process — "the same machine" that nearly buried them now serving, through a different door, to save them. He explicitly and deliberately hands off the two threads that exceed what "two people on a bench" can responsibly pursue: (a) **the Altera-board director's identity** — now formally a question for the Technical Council's investigation, with subpoena power and financial-records access neither of them has or should try to replicate; and (b) **his own Technist-era account** with Rational Innovation's playbook — referenced only obliquely ("an account she had never seen the opening of, closing now in the ordinary afternoon"), explicitly not explained on-page, and explicitly not pursued by Yara ("you earned letting it" / "don't tell anyone"). At the foot of the terraces, Del peels off toward the Authority to begin his own reinstatement process; Yara watches him go, understanding it as the same trust he showed letting her face Nwosu alone.
9. **Late afternoon into early evening, ~Hour 104–106** — Alone, Yara returns to Station Seven. She runs the same sensor check she ran in the book's opening chapter — the same motion, the same hands — and finds, for the first time in six days, that the smell and the manual-gauge numbers finally agree: "the lie gone out from between them." She makes the closing log entry on the station terminal that "answered to her again" — plain, factual, true: the date, the station, the manual sequence walked, the readings recovering, the system returned to certified function. She closes the terminal, breathes the station's air, shoulders the kit, and walks the wetland path home in the late afternoon light as the city's terraces begin to warm their lamps against the coming dark. **Book 1 ends here.**

## Open time pressure — RESOLVED

The bloom clock that drove the entire book's tension is now closed. Containment was achieved by manual intervention roughly **14–20 hours before** chemical irreversibility (Hour 114), and confirmed independently by the Council's own emergency-response assessment as the only reason the situation remained "recoverable and not a loss." 

**Both parallel clocks referenced at Chapter 5's close — the chemical countdown and the Compact's institutional-response window — resolved successfully and in the story's favor, on-page, within the same single day (Day 4).** Neither ran out. The chemical clock stopped mattering once the manual sequence took hold; the institutional clock produced its full cascade (frozen contracts, opened review, resignation, denial) well within the same morning it was triggered — faster, in fact, than anyone "on the inside" had ever moved.
