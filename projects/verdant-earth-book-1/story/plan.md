# Chapter Plan — The Mycelial City

## Chapter 1: Station Seven

**Target word count**: 3,500–4,000 words
**Primary POV location**: The eastern inlet biofilter wetlands → Water Authority HQ

### Scene sequence

**1A — Opening at Station Seven** (~800w)
Yara does a routine inspection of Station Seven, eastern inlet. Establish New Accra at dawn through her expert perception: the smells she reads like diagnostics, the city visible above the wetlands, the specific texture of a living system doing its job. She runs a sensor check. The numbers are wrong — not drastically, but in a pattern. She notes it, takes physical samples, adds a maintenance flag.

**1B — Back at the Authority** (~600w)
She checks Station Seven's data against the wider network from her office. The anomaly is consistent across three inlet readings but doesn't show up in the regional summary feed. That discrepancy is the first signal that this is strange.

**1C — Filing the report** (~700w)
Yara writes a preliminary anomaly report and puts it into the queue for Director Nwosu's review. Introduce Nwosu briefly — competent, cautious, reputation-conscious. Yara expects routine acknowledgment.

**1D — First encounter with Del** (~900w)
Del Marchetti stops by Yara's office at end of day — he's been reviewing the Accord's independent sensor feed and noticed an anomaly in the same stations, slightly different data set. They compare notes. Neither has an explanation. Del wants to meet tomorrow. Introduce their dynamic: his institutional pattern-reading vs. her technical specificity.

**1E — Chapter close** (~500w)
Yara walks the public wetland path home. The city at dusk. She checks the Station Seven samples on her tablet. The numbers are still wrong. She files an urgent flag on the report.

**Chapter ending**: She refreshes the queue. The report status reads: *No record found.*

## Chapter 2: The Missing Report

**Target word count**: 3,500–4,000 words
**Primary POV location**: Water Authority HQ → Director Nwosu's office → Del's Accord office

### Scene sequence

**2A — The empty queue** (~600w)
Yara confirms the report is gone. Not filed incorrectly — the system shows she never submitted one. She has the draft. The submission timestamp is in her own log. Someone deleted it after it was received.

**2B — Nwosu meeting** (~900w)
Yara goes directly to Nwosu. Nwosu acknowledges the anomaly with careful concern — yes, she's seen the readings, yes, it's being reviewed by internal review, no, Yara doesn't need to be involved further. The Authority has protocols. Yara presses; Nwosu gives her the professional sideline: reassurance that functions as removal. Yara is formally rotated off eastern inlet monitoring.

**2C — Yara and Del compare notes** (~700w)
Del has also been noticed: the Accord monitoring dashboard now shows the eastern inlet readings as "under scheduled maintenance." He and Yara work through what they have independently. The patterns are the same. The suppression is coordinated, not accidental.

**2D — First biological stakes** (~800w)
Yara runs projections from the raw data she still has. The anomaly pattern is consistent with early-stage cyanobacterial bloom formation — a process the biofilter should be suppressing but appears to be allowing through. If it continues, the bloom will become chemically self-sustaining. She calculates time to irreversibility. The number she gets is uncomfortable.

**2E — Chapter close** (~500w)
Del asks: what do we do? Yara has the answer she's not supposed to have: they keep going, off-shift, with what they already have access to.

**Chapter ending**: "If someone deleted that report, they know what I found. They know I'll find it again."

## Chapter 3: The Pattern

**Target word count**: 3,500–4,500 words
**Primary POV location**: Del's office → Station 7, 8, 9 physical inspections → Water Authority archive

### Scene sequence

**3A — Night inspection** (~900w)
Yara, off-shift and off-rotation, visits the inlet stations physically. She has personal credentials that haven't been revoked (they rotated her off monitoring assignment, not off employee access). She's looking for what the sensors can't tell her. At Station Eight, she finds a hardware anomaly: a firmware sync tag that shouldn't be in the physical log. She photographs it.

**3B — Del traces the certification chain** (~900w)
Del uses his Accord credentials to pull historical certification records for the inlet stations' biotech nodes. Three of them were recertified four months ago by a firm called Altera Technical Services. The recertification passed at the Accord level. He looks closer at the certifying analyst.

**3C — Finding the firmware vector** (~800w)
Yara and Del work together on the firmware comparison. Yara builds a model of what the modified firmware is doing: not disabling the sensors — masking specific chemical signatures within a defined range. Whoever did this expected the bloom to develop slowly enough that the masking would hold until it was too late to stop.

**3D — Red herring: the equipment supplier** (~600w)
The hardware for Stations 7 and 8 came from a different supplier than Station 9 — Yara flags this as a possible vector. They spend time on it. By the end of the chapter, the supplier is ruled out: the hardware is standard, unmodified. The firmware was installed after delivery. *This was deliberate, not opportunistic.*

**3E — Chapter close** (~400w)
They have a vector: the firmware. They have a window: four days. They don't yet have who.

**Chapter ending**: Del pulls up Altera Technical Services' corporate registration. The sole director's other listed directorships include a board seat at *Rational Innovation — Infrastructure Advisory Group.*

## Chapter 4: The Institution Behind It

**Target word count**: 4,000–4,500 words
**Primary POV location**: Del's office → Accord records → physical city (the beautiful scene) → closing confrontation

### Scene sequence

**4A — Rational Innovation** (~900w)
Del knows Rational Innovation: a Technist lobbying organization with a sophisticated public-interest argument (biotech certification is too restrictive, slowing beneficial innovation). He walks Yara through what they actually do. They've been pushing for looser certification rules in three jurisdictions. New Accra is a showcase — if New Accra's ecological management looks vulnerable, their regulatory argument gets stronger.

**4B — The mechanism** (~800w)
They reconstruct the play: Altera modifies certification firmware in key monitoring stations. Bloom develops. Authority can't see it coming. Emergency declared, remediation required. *Rational Innovation* publishes a report on the failure. "If the certified biotech was this fallible, the certification system itself needs reform." The sabotage isn't destruction — it's a demonstration.

**4C — The city at midday** (~600w)
Yara takes the wetland path at noon. She describes the city the way she sees it — not as an argument, but as the thing. What it smells like. What it sounds like. What it would look like if the bloom reached the public wetlands. This is the scene that makes the stakes personal. The city has done everything right. The vulnerability isn't ecological — it's institutional.

**4D — Assembling the escalation** (~800w)
They draft the formal escalation: firmware comparison, Altera certification records, Rational Innovation connection, bloom projection. Del walks through the official path: it goes to the Technical Council, triggers an automatic inspection, and requires immediate response. Normally five days. They have less than that. He contacts his Accord supervisor.

**4E — The suspension** (~600w)
The response comes faster than expected. Yara is formally suspended pending review — conflict of interest, for having originally filed the report under investigation. Del's secondment credentials are frozen by the same mechanism. They're procedurally removed from the process.

**Chapter ending**: Del is very calm. "They know what we have. They moved to contain us before we could file it."

## Chapter 5: Outside the System

**Target word count**: 3,500–4,000 words
**Primary POV location**: Yara's apartment → public spaces → closed meeting → night

### Scene sequence

**5A — Yara alone** (~700w)
Morning after the suspension. Yara is off the network, out of the building, officially uninvolved. She makes coffee. She checks the raw data she still has locally — the bloom projection is now forty-eight hours to chemical irreversibility. She is precise and calm in the way of someone who has decided to keep working.

**5B — What they can still do** (~800w)
Yara and Del meet outside (a public space; they have no offices to use). Inventory: they have all the data they collected before the credentials were frozen. They have Yara's personal equipment log from the physical inspections. They have the firmware comparison. They don't have official standing. Del explains the Open Knowledge Compact: Covenant-era right of public access to ecological monitoring data. If the data is hers — collected in her professional capacity on a public infrastructure system — she has the right to publish it.

**5C — The saboteur has a face** (~600w)
Del has been working his personal network. The certifying analyst at Altera who signed the firmware recertification is a former Technical Council employee — fired two years ago following a standards disagreement. A connection, not a conspiracy, but a human one. Del finds this is useful to know.

**5D — Attempted contact** (~700w)
Yara attempts to reach Nwosu outside official channels. Brief, difficult conversation. Nwosu knows what's happening; she's protecting something — the city's reputation, the review process, herself, maybe all three. She isn't corrupt. She's afraid of what a confirmed sabotage finding means for New Accra's image. Yara makes one direct statement and ends the call.

**5E — Preparation** (~500w)
They prepare the publication package. Yara confirms her personal Compact credentials are untouched. She reads the Compact's public-deposit procedure carefully. This is not a leak; it is a right.

**Chapter ending**: She sets the upload to go at 5 AM, when system traffic is lowest and verification can begin before the work day starts. She doesn't sleep.

## Chapter 6: The Open Record

**Target word count**: 3,500–4,000 words
**Primary POV location**: Yara's apartment → wetland path → Authority HQ → Station Seven

### Scene sequence

**6A — Publication** (~600w)
5 AM. The upload completes. The Open Knowledge Compact archive registers the deposit. Yara watches the verification tracker. The first independent researcher pings the data within twenty minutes — a water systems academic in Nairobi, running a sanity check. It's real. It's public.

**6B — Cascade** (~700w)
By 7 AM, the Accord Technical Council has received automated notification of the public deposit. Their mandate requires a response within six hours. The New Accra Water Authority receives a mandatory inspection order. Del's secondment credentials are quietly unfrozen — someone in the Technical Council hierarchy is already trying to get ahead of this.

**6C — The bloom** (~800w)
Yara doesn't wait. She goes to Station Seven herself. With the bloom projections she has and the hours remaining, someone needs to be physically present to initiate manual biofilter override — a procedure that doesn't require credentials, just physical access and technical knowledge. She spends two hours in the wetlands doing the kind of detailed, unglamorous remediation work that she does well.

**6D — Resolution** (~700w)
The Technical Council's emergency team arrives at noon. The manual work Yara did bought time; the emergency biofilter restoration is technically viable. The bloom is contained. Narrowly. Del arrives and tells her what's happening at the institutional level: Altera's contracts are frozen pending investigation, Nwosu has resigned, the certification review has begun. *Rational Innovation* has issued a denial.

**6E — The city, and what remains** (~700w)
Late afternoon. Yara and Del walk the wetland path. Del talks through what the investigation will look like, who will be accountable, how long it will take. Yara listens. The city is visible, doing what it does. She knows the bloom will leave a trace in the eastern inlet data record for years — evidence of what almost happened.

**6F — Final scene** (~300w)
Yara is at Station Seven, alone. She runs the sensor check. The numbers are right. She makes a log entry — routine, factual, professional. She takes a breath of the station's air: green, wet, mineral, alive. She walks back toward the city.

**Chapter ending**: Soft landing. The thing was worth protecting. It still is.
