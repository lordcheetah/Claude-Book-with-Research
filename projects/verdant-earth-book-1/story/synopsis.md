# Synopsis — The Mycelial City

## Logline
In 2091's New Accra, a water systems engineer discovers a pattern in the biofilter network that shouldn't exist — and follows it into an institutional cover-up that risks the city's ecological infrastructure and her career.

---

## Back-cover blurb
New Accra, 2091. The city works. Its biofilter wetlands clean the water, its mycelial networks track every pipe and pond, and Yara Osei has spent ten years making sure the system stays that way.

Then Station Seven's numbers start lying.

The readings are wrong in a pattern Yara can't explain — not a calibration error, not equipment failure. Something has been modified in the biotech certification firmware, and whatever it is, someone at the Water Authority wants it to stay invisible. When Yara's anomaly report disappears before it leaves her department, she knows two things: the algal bloom forming in the eastern inlet will be irreversible in four days, and the only person she trusts is a visiting Accord analyst named Del Marchetti who talks too much and knows where every body is buried.

*The Mycelial City* is a procedural thriller about what it costs to tell the truth inside the institutions built to protect it — set in a world that has already done the hard work of becoming worth protecting.

## Full synopsis

### Setup
Yara Osei is a water systems engineer at the New Accra Water Authority, ten years into a career maintaining the biofilter network that keeps one of the world's most-watched post-Collapse cities ecologically functional. She is good at her job in the specific way that matters: she can read sensor logs the way a musician reads notation, and she trusts what the data tells her.

On a routine inspection of Station Seven, she notices an anomaly. The mycorrhizal readings don't match the biochemical outputs — the numbers are wrong in a way that rules out equipment malfunction. Yara files a preliminary report and begins investigating.

Before the report reaches her supervisor, Director Adaeze Nwosu, it disappears from the system queue.

### Escalation
Yara escalates to Nwosu directly. Nwosu acknowledges the anomaly, assures Yara it's being handled, and removes her from the monitoring rotation for the eastern inlet. It's a professional sideline dressed as reassurance, and Yara recognizes it.

She is not removed from her job. She simply no longer officially has access to the stations she's been monitoring for years.

She continues working the problem off-shift, with Del Marchetti — an Accord Technical Council systems analyst on a two-year New Accra secondment who has been quietly following the same unusual numbers from the Accord side. Del brings institutional literacy; Yara brings technical expertise. Together they trace the anomaly from symptoms to source: modified certification firmware in three of the biotech monitoring nodes, allowing readings to be selectively masked.

### Stakes and complications
The masked readings are covering the early formation of an algal bloom in the eastern inlet — a cyanobacterial expansion that will become chemically irreversible within four days if the biofilter suppression continues. If it reaches that point, the Water Authority will need to declare an ecological emergency that will take months to remediate and will end careers, including Nwosu's.

Del and Yara identify the firmware modification trail. It leads to a technical consulting firm that holds Accord certification contracts for several West African cities — a firm with documented ties to the Technist lobbying organization *Rational Innovation*, which has been pushing for less restrictive biotech regulation for three years.

When Yara prepares to send a formal escalation to the Accord Technical Council directly, she is placed on formal suspension — conflict of interest, for having filed the original report now under review. Del's secondment credentials are frozen the same day, preventing him from acting in any official capacity.

They have access to everything they've already found. They cannot officially use it.

### Resolution
The Open Knowledge Compact — a Covenant-era commitment to public access for ecological monitoring data — gives Yara a path. She publishes the complete technical record: sensor logs, firmware comparison, bloom data, correspondence timestamps showing when the report disappeared. Not through the Water Authority. Directly, to the Compact's public archive, from her personal credentials that the suspension order did not revoke.

The publication is public within two hours. Independent verification begins within four. The Accord Technical Council is forced into emergency response: mandatory inspection of all certification firmware in New Accra's network, and emergency biofilter restoration in the eastern inlet.

The bloom is contained. Narrowly.

Director Nwosu resigns in the internal process that follows — she was protecting the city's reputation, not participating in the sabotage, and the distinction matters. The consulting firm loses its certification contracts. *Rational Innovation* issues a statement denying involvement; the investigation continues.

Yara's suspension is lifted. Del's credentials are unfrozen. Neither is exactly celebrated.

The last scene: Yara at Station Seven, running the numbers. They're right. She makes a note in the log, the way she always does. The city continues.

## Themes
- Systems as things worth caring about
- Institutional trust and its limits — when the process is the obstacle
- The partial win: not everything is fixed, but this specific thing is
- Competence as a form of integrity

---

## Series note
This book establishes the Verdant Earth world from the ground level. It deliberately avoids introducing any world-level threats or metaplot — the story is local, procedural, and resolved. Book 2 will follow a different protagonist in the same world.
