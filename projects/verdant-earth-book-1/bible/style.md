# Style Guide — The Mycelial City

## Narrative voice

### Point of view
- [x] Third person limited (focused on: Yara Osei)

### Tense
- Primary tense: past simple
- Secondary tenses: past perfect for flashbacks; present for Yara's direct internal observations (in italics)

### Narrator tone
Close third — inside Yara's head without disappearing into stream of consciousness. Observational, technically precise when describing systems, emotionally restrained in the way of someone who expresses care through competence rather than sentiment. Dry, understated humor in moments of frustration. Never ironic about the world itself — solarpunk's optimism is sincere, not wry.

---

## Language & vocabulary

### Register
Standard. Technical vocabulary used accurately and without apology — Yara is an expert and the reader is trusted to follow. New terms are introduced in context, not defined in asides.

### Era-appropriate language
- Target era: 2091, West African coast / global cooperative network
- Ecological Covenant vocabulary used naturally: "Restoration Credits," "bioregional," "the Accord," "Covenant-certified"
- Forbidden: anachronistic negativity about ecological technology (characters in 2091 don't think of clean energy as unusual or idealistic)
- Encouraged: technical precision about biofilter systems, mycelial networks, water chemistry; the sensory texture of a living city

### Expressions
- Characters use contemporary professional register, not futuristic slang
- West African English cadences inform Yara's internal voice — not dialect, but rhythm
- "The Before Time" — how older characters refer to the pre-Covenant era (used sparingly, not as a tagline)

---

## Sentence structure

### Average length
- Target: 14–22 words
- Maximum: 38 words (longer sentences for technical detail or emotional weight)

### Complexity
- [x] Simple sentences in action and tension
- [x] Compound sentences in dialogue and technical explanation
- Nested clauses allowed in Yara's analytical observations

### Rhythm
Precise and varied. Technical passages use shorter, declarative sentences. Character moments allow longer, more recursive structure. Scene transitions are crisp — no lingering.

---

## Paragraphs & chapters

### Paragraph length
- Average: 3–4 sentences
- Maximum: 6 sentences

### Chapter length
- Target: 3,000–4,500 words (short test novel — 6 chapters total)
- Structure: Scene-sequel. Each chapter advances the investigation and raises the stakes.

### Chapter endings
- [x] Cliffhangers for action/discovery chapters
- [x] Open questions for investigation chapters
- Soft landing allowed only for the final chapter

---

## Dialogue

### Formatting
- Quotation style: " " (standard English)
- Attribution: after dialogue when possible; action beats over tags when characters move

### Tags
Preferred: said, asked, replied, added, noted, cut in, continued
Forbidden: hissed, spat, breathed (as speech tag)

### Balance
- Dialogue to prose ratio: ~30% — this is an investigation story, internal analysis carries more weight than conversation

---

## Description

### Sensory details
Priority: visual + olfactory (the living city has distinctive smells — green, wet, mineral, alive)
Touch when relevant to systems work (valves, panels, water)

### Length
- Scene setting: moderate — 3–5 sentences establishing the feel of a location
- Character description: minimal, functional
- Technical systems: detailed when they're central to the plot; brief otherwise

### Techniques
- [x] Similes grounded in Yara's engineering knowledge
- Technical precision as a form of description (the exact name of a system is more evocative than a vague gesture)

---

## Tone & mood

### Overall tone
Purposeful. A world that works, being threatened by something that shouldn't be happening. The mood is investigative tension within a backdrop of genuine beauty — New Accra is a good place and its vulnerability is the stakes.

### Emotional range
Determination, frustration, wonder (at the city and its systems), betrayal, satisfaction. No despair — solarpunk doesn't go there.

### Humor
Dry professional humor; Yara's sardonic internal commentary on bureaucratic obstruction.

---

## Forbidden elements

### Content
- No gratuitous violence (this is a thriller, not action)
- No cynicism about ecological restoration or cooperative governance (characters may be frustrated by institutions, never dismissive of the project)

### Style
- No "info-dumping" exposition about the world — all worldbuilding through action and observation
- No characters explaining the Ecological Covenant to each other as if they don't know it
- No modern-day dystopian framing — this world is genuinely better, not secretly terrible

---

## Genre conventions

### Solarpunk
- The world is genuinely better — not ironically, not secretly. Characters live inside the Covenant without explaining it.
- Solutions are collective and procedural, not individual heroism. The win at the end is partial and honest about that.
- Labor and cooperation are the engine of the world. Show people doing skilled work and taking it seriously.
- Technology is integrated with ecology, not opposed to it. Biotech is infrastructure, not spectacle.
- Optimism is load-bearing, not naive. The story acknowledges what was lost; it doesn't pretend the Collapse didn't happen.

### Procedural thriller
- The investigative logic must be airtight. Every clue Yara finds should be discoverable by the reader from the available evidence.
- Fair-play convention: no solutions that depend on information the reader didn't have access to.
- Pacing: each chapter should advance the investigation AND raise a new obstacle or question. Standing still is not allowed.
- The antagonist is an institution, not a person — the "villain" is a structural incentive, not individual malice. The story should never simplify this.
- Technical accuracy matters. The biofilter, mycorrhizal systems, and water chemistry must behave as real systems would.

### What this is NOT
- Not a thriller where the protagonist is exceptional or uniquely heroic — Yara wins by doing her job correctly
- Not dystopian — institutional frustration is not institutional condemnation
- Not a heist or action story — no physical confrontation, no chase sequences
