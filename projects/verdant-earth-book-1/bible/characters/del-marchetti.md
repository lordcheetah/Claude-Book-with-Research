# Del Marchetti

## Identity
- Full name: Delio Marchetti
- Nickname(s): Del (universal; he introduced himself this way on day one)
- Age: 41
- Role in story: Deuteragonist / ally

---

## Appearance
- Physical description: Tall, slightly rumpled, the look of someone who owns good clothes and doesn't quite maintain them. Brown skin, grey beginning at his temples earlier than expected.
- Distinctive features: Gestures when he talks — hands describe arcs that sometimes match what he's saying
- Typical clothing: Accord technical staff uniform (the equivalent of business casual in 2091); jacket always slightly wrinkled

---

## Personality

### Core traits
- **Institutional fluency**: Del understands how organizations work — where the real power is, whose sign-off actually matters, which rule is actually enforced. He uses this to navigate, not to exploit.
- **Convert's conscientiousness**: He came from a Technist-leaning family and chose the cooperative path as an adult. He takes it more seriously than people who were born into it, in the way converts sometimes do.
- **Verbal abundance**: He talks through problems. His thinking happens out loud, which is either charming or exhausting depending on your tolerance.

### Strengths
- Political and institutional map-reading — he knows where the bodies are buried
- Can translate between technical expertise and institutional language
- Catches the human angle that Yara misses

### Flaws
- Tends to over-explain; sometimes takes three sentences to say what one would do
- His institutional fluency occasionally tips into over-caution — he knows too many ways things can go wrong

### Fears
- Being wrong in a way that compromises someone he's vouched for
- That his Technist background will be used against him at a critical moment

### Desires
- To be useful; to have his cooperation mean something beyond compliance

---

## Background
Born in São Paulo to an Italian-Brazilian father who worked in resource extraction consulting and a Brazilian mother who was an Accord compliance officer. He spent his twenties in Technist-adjacent consulting, left when a client asked him to certify something he couldn't certify, and spent his thirties rebuilding a career inside Accord institutions. He's been in New Accra for two years on a Technical Council secondment.

---

## Relationships
- **Yara Osei**: He respects her expertise completely and finds her institutional blind spots both admirable and maddening. He thinks she's right more often than she gives herself credit for.
- **The Technical Council**: His employer and his primary source of professional tension. He believes in what it's supposed to do; he's clear-eyed about how it actually functions.

---

## Speech patterns

### Voice characteristics
- Tone: Warm, slightly wry, occasionally exasperated
- Register: Professional but conversational
- Pace: Quick; he fills silence

### Verbal tics & expressions
- "Here's the thing" — signals he's about to complicate something
- "Technically speaking, and I want to emphasize the word technically..."
- Names the institution before criticizing it: "The Council, which I love and serve faithfully, is going to —"

### Dialogue examples
> "The good news is that our findings are correct. The bad news is that being correct isn't sufficient. The Technical Council will want independent verification, which takes time we don't have, and an internal review, which will take longer."

> "Here's the thing about the Accord's certification process: it was designed by people who assumed everyone was acting in good faith. Which is charming, really."

> "Yara. I know you know I know you're right. Can we talk about what we're going to do about it?"

---

## Behavior patterns

### Under stress
- Talks more, not less; works through anxiety verbally
- Starts mapping the institutional landscape — who can do what, who owes whom
- Writes lists; physical act of writing calms him

### In conflict
- Tries to find the procedural path first; not a confrontational person
- When backed into a corner, is more decisive than he looks

### When happy
- Expansive; tells stories about other things
- Makes bad food recommendations with great confidence

---

## Connection to Rational Innovation

In his twenties, during his Technist-adjacent consulting period, Del encountered Rational Innovation directly — they were one of the organizations pushing deregulation of biotech certification standards, and some of his work intersected with their lobbying interests. He wasn't a true believer, but he was in those rooms, understood their argument, and recognized their tactics. He left that world shortly after; Rational Innovation was part of the landscape he walked away from.

When their name appears in Chapter 3, he recognizes it immediately. "I've heard of them, distantly" is temporal (fifteen years ago) and deliberate understatement. He knows what they actually do beneath the public-interest language about certification reform. He doesn't say more because naming them fully would require explaining why he knows — and his Technist background is something he carries quietly.

His personal stake in Chapter 4: some part of what he's doing is finishing a moral accounting that started when he chose to leave. He walked away from that world; he is now, for the first time, actively working against it. This shouldn't be stated directly in the story — it should be the subtext under his particular thoroughness in Chapter 4.

**How it surfaces in Ch. 4**: When Del explains Rational Innovation to Yara, he knows their talking points by heart — he heard them for years. The institutional-insider fluency becomes something more uncomfortable: he's not translating, he's recognizing. The arc reviewer's note applies: this is not a dramatic reveal, it's a note in the room.
