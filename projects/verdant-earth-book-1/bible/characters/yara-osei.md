# Yara Osei

## Identity
- Full name: Yara Adwoa Osei
- Nickname(s): None — she uses her full first name
- Age: 34
- Role in story: Protagonist

---

## Appearance
- Physical description: Medium build, compact and physical from a career that involves crawling through maintenance spaces. Natural hair worn close-cropped for practicality. Hands that look like they do real work.
- Distinctive features: A habit of going very still when she's thinking — people sometimes think she's checked out; she hasn't.
- Typical clothing: Work coveralls in the field; clean professional clothes for meetings (she dislikes meetings)

---

## Personality

### Core traits (permanent)
- **Competence as care**: Yara shows she cares about something by doing it well. She doesn't express feelings easily but maintains the biofilter network the way some people maintain relationships.
- **Empirical stubbornness**: She believes what the data says, and she won't be argued out of it by authority or convenience. This is her greatest strength and the thing that gets her into trouble.
- **Protective instinct (systems)**: She feels responsible for the systems under her care in a way that's almost personal. The New Accra biofilter network is, to her, a living thing worth protecting.

### Strengths
- Water systems expertise; can read a sensor log the way a musician reads notation
- Pattern recognition — she notices what's wrong before she can articulate why
- Calm under operational pressure (the crisis version of her is better than the normal version)
- Knows how to work within institutions and when to stop

### Flaws
- Poor at institutional politics; she underestimates how much people protect turf
- Doesn't ask for help easily — will work a problem alone past the point where she should involve others
- Impatient with process when the problem is visible and the solution is obvious (to her)

### Fears
- Being responsible for a preventable failure — this is the nightmare
- That the systems she maintains are more fragile than she tells herself

### Desires
- To keep New Accra's water clean, which is not a small thing
- To understand what she's looking at — she can't leave an anomaly alone

---

## Background
Yara grew up in the Kumasi Cooperative, the daughter of parents who relocated from coastal flooding in the 2060s — old enough to have heard stories of the Before Time, young enough to have grown up entirely inside the Covenant world. She studied water systems engineering at the New Accra Technical Institute on a cooperative scholarship. She has been with the New Accra Water Authority for ten years. She is good at her job and knows it, which is different from arrogance.

She lives alone. She has a small courtyard garden that she maintains with the same focused attention she brings to the biofilter network. She talks to her mother every week.

---

## Relationships
- **Del Marchetti**: Colleague and ally. She trusts him because he's been wrong in interesting ways — he catches things she misses, and vice versa. Their banter is professional and affectionate without being romantic.
- **Director Adaeze Nwosu** (Water Authority): Yara's supervisor. Competent, cautious, and susceptible to political pressure — Yara respects her capabilities and is frustrated by her caution.
- **The biofilter network**: Not a character, but treated by Yara with something close to relationship. She knows its rhythms.

---

## Speech patterns

### Voice characteristics
- Tone: Precise, dry, direct
- Register: Professional; drops into technical vocabulary naturally
- Pace: Measured; she doesn't rush her sentences

### Verbal tics & expressions
- States conclusions before evidence when she's certain: "The firmware was modified. Here's how I know."
- Uses "interesting" as a warning sign — when she says a reading is "interesting," Del has learned to pay attention
- Rarely uses intensifiers; when she says something "matters," it does

### Dialogue examples
> "Station Seven's numbers are wrong. Not out-of-calibration wrong — wrong in a pattern."

> "I'm not saying someone did this on purpose. I'm saying the data doesn't support any explanation that doesn't involve someone doing this on purpose."

> "Del, how long has the Technical Council been doing things on my schedule?"
> A pause. "I see your point."

---

## Behavior patterns

### Under stress
- Goes quiet and focused; the external calm is real but doesn't mean she isn't worried
- Works through the problem methodically even when she wants to rush
- Sleeps badly; wakes early and checks readings from her tablet in the dark

### In conflict
- States her position once, clearly
- Does not repeat herself to people who are not listening
- Will route around an obstacle rather than argue with it — except when the obstacle is actually preventing her from fixing the problem

### When happy
- Rare and private; a contained satisfaction
- The quiet pleasure of a system running exactly as it should

---

## Arc potential
Yara begins as someone who believes the systems are enough — that good technical work, honestly done, will be protected by the institutions that exist to protect it. The story tests this belief. She ends it knowing the systems need defending, not just maintaining.

---

## Notes for writer
- Her expertise should feel real: she notices things about water and biological systems that other characters don't
- She doesn't editorialize about the Covenant or solarpunk values — she just lives inside them
- Her relationship with the biofilter network is the emotional engine of the book; her care for it should be legible without being stated
- She and Del have an easy rapport built on mutual professional respect — their scenes should feel like people who are good at working together
