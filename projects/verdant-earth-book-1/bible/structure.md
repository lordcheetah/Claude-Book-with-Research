# Story Structure — The Mycelial City

## Narrative arc (6-chapter short novel)

### Act 1 — Setup (chapters 1–2)
- Establish New Accra and Yara's world — a beautiful, functional solarpunk city
- The biofilter anomaly surfaces: sensor readings that shouldn't exist
- Inciting incident: Yara's anomaly report is suppressed before it leaves her department

### Act 2A — Investigation (chapters 3–4)
- Yara and Del dig deeper; the failure isn't random — it's patterned
- They identify the sabotage vector: modified certification firmware in the biotech nodes
- Stakes raised: the algal bloom will become irreversible within days
- A Technist lobbying organization appears in the money trail

### Act 2B — Complication (chapter 5)
- Yara is officially suspended from the investigation (conflict of interest — she filed the original report)
- Del's Accord credentials are frozen pending review
- They work from outside the system with what they already have
- The saboteur is identified — not a villain but an institution making a calculated bet

### Act 3 — Resolution (chapter 6)
- Yara uses the Open Knowledge Compact to publish the full technical record directly, bypassing the suppression chain
- The Accord Technical Council is forced to act publicly
- The bloom is contained — narrowly
- The Technist organization doesn't collapse, but this particular play fails
- Yara and Del: what they've each learned, and what's still broken

## Chapter patterns

### Typical structure
1. Opening in a specific physical location (Yara always grounded in place)
2. Technical discovery or investigation advance
3. Human complication (obstruction, alliance, new information from a person)
4. Ending on an escalating question or a door closing

### Recurring elements
- [x] Yara interacts with a living system before making a decision
- [x] The city itself is described once per chapter — its beauty is load-bearing
- [x] Del provides institutional/political context Yara lacks

---

## Mystery mechanics

### Clue distribution
- Chapters 1–2: The anomaly and its first-order explanation (equipment fault)
- Chapters 3–4: The pattern that rules out equipment fault; the firmware vector
- Chapter 5: The money trail and the institution behind it
- Chapter 6: The final confirmation and the public record

### Red herrings
One: an equipment supplier whose readings look suspicious but whose involvement is circumstantial (ruled out chapter 4).

### Resolution pattern
Technical and procedural — the sabotage is defeated by transparency and open publication, not confrontation. The antagonist is an institution, not a person; the win is partial and honest about that.

---

## Tension management

### Build-up
- The ecological clock (bloom irreversibility) creates time pressure from chapter 2 onward
- Institutional obstruction escalates each chapter
- Yara's personal stakes increase as she risks her career and Del's secondment

### Release valves
- One scene per act showing the city functioning beautifully — what's at stake, made visible
- Del's humor deflates the most oppressive moments
