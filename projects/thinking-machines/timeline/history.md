## Chapter 1 - Learning Without Instructions
- [Established: rules-vs-examples distinction, via spam-filter throughline — early rule-based filters collapsed because fixed lists can always be routed around; the alternative is showing a system labeled examples and letting it find the pattern itself]
- [Established: weights/parameters as adjustable settings — "a control panel with a million dials" analogy, doing double duty: makes the concept concrete AND dismantles the "weights are exotic/magical" misconception (novelty is scale, not kind)]
- [Established: the training loop — guess, measure (loss function), adjust (gradient descent), repeat — "a skier descending a mountain in fog" analogy, explicitly flagged in-text to resurface in later chapters]
- [Corrected: three misconceptions — (1) the system was shown, not told; (2) weights aren't magical, they're ordinary numbers at extraordinary scale; (3) gradient descent finds a good-enough locally-reachable answer, not the single theoretically-best one]
- [Planted: the transparency problem is structural, not a flaw or oversight — a system that finds its own patterns will necessarily hold patterns nobody can fully read back out; "powerful" and "hard to explain" reframed as one property seen from two angles — seed for Chapter 7]
- [Planted: "powerful but imperfect optimizer" thread (skier-in-fog) — explicitly marked in the chapter's own text as something that "will resurface, in different shapes, more than once before this book is done" — likely payoff in Chapter 6 (failure modes/hallucination) and possibly Chapter 8 (alignment)]
- [Bridged to: Chapter 2's question — nothing in the spam-filter story is specific to email or two categories; the same guess/measure/adjust loop, at far greater scale, underlies systems that converse, code, and explain. So: what, mechanically, is a system doing, moment to moment, when it produces the next word of a sentence?]

## Chapter 2 - Reading as Prediction
- [Established: text reaches and leaves the system as tokens, not words — "a kit of interlocking LEGO-style pieces" analogy explains tokenization concretely and corrects the "strawberry" letter-counting misconception in the same stroke (a system may never see "strawberry" as eight letters, only as whatever pieces its kit breaks it into)]
- [Established: next-token prediction as the entire pretraining objective — stated with full, unhedged confidence per the research brief's instruction that this ground is settled. Not "understand," not "know what's true," not "be helpful." One task, repeated an almost incomprehensible number of times, across nearly everything humans have digitized and written down]
- [Established: the "Paris" mechanism — fluent, correct-sounding output is produced via statistical pattern density, not stored-fact lookup or retrieval. "Paris" isn't an answer the system looked up; it's the single most likely next token, by an overwhelming margin, given a pattern repeated across thousands of independent sources]
- [Corrected: "the model looks things up / retrieves facts like a search engine or database" — dismantled directly and by name via the "Paris" walkthrough, replaced with the actual mechanism (pattern density, not retrieval)]
- [Named fairly, deliberately left open: the "stochastic parrots" debate — one serious camp (Bender, Mitchell, et al., 2021) holding that fluency is not understanding, full stop; another holding that this framing increasingly strains against what these systems visibly do. The book explicitly commits to NOT resolving this — "the result *looks like* knowing... nobody currently knows" — and later chapters (5, 8) must preserve this live, unresolved, both-serious framing]
- [Planted: the structural sounds-true/is-true gap — a system optimized purely for "sounds right" has no built-in way to distinguish that from "is right," identified as a *necessary* structural consequence of the training objective (not a flaw, not half-finished engineering), stated in the same even register Chapter 1 used for the transparency problem — seed for Chapter 6 (hallucination)]
- [New analogies deployed: phone autocomplete "scaled roughly a billion-fold" (the chapter's anchor, doing for Ch.2 what the spam filter did for Ch.1); crayon-drawing-of-a-coastline vs. satellite-photograph (the qualitative leap from small to large, channeling "novelty is scale, not kind"); LEGO-kit of interlocking pieces (tokenization, load-bearing for Chapter 3); the million-dial control panel from Ch.1 recalled and explicitly multiplied to billions]
- [Bridged to: Chapter 3's question — a pile of correctly-predicted tokens isn't, by itself, meaning; producing "Paris" in the right place isn't the same as the concept of Paris meaning anything to anything. So: if prediction is the *task* — and we now know, in detail, that it is — what is the system actually *holding*, internally, that makes its predictions this good? What does "the capital of France is Paris" look like inside something that has never seen a map, a city, or a capital of any kind?]

## Chapter 3 - Representation: A Map With No Master Plan
- [Established: representation as position — what the system holds internally is not facts or records but *position*; every token corresponds to a specific point ("an embedding," defined plainly as a coordinate-list) in an enormous structured space, where geometric closeness genuinely tracks conceptual closeness. Stated with full, unhedged confidence, explicitly calibrated to match Chapter 2's "next-token prediction is the entire training objective"]
- [Established: the "map where every concept has an address" analogy, with its caveat (messier than any real map) front-loaded rather than appended — the chapter's central organizing image, expected to carry heavy load through Chapters 4–5]
- [Corrected — carefully scoped: the famous "king − man + woman ≈ queen" vector-arithmetic demonstration, presented at full strength, checked live on the page ("has anyone gone back and checked it?"), and found not to survive in its clean, popularly-presented form — attributed to "one detailed review" that called the popular version "fake news." The correction was explicitly and precisely scoped to the *demonstration's mechanics* (an undisclosed exclusion of query words from candidate answers), not to the underlying idea]
- [Established — what survives the correction, stated with equal and explicit unhedged confidence: relationships between concepts really are encoded as directions and distances in this space — "settled... foundational... every bit as solid as the first claim." The chapter modeled, live, the difference between dismantling a flashy demonstration and dismantling the mechanism beneath it — and named this distinction as the single most important sentence-level move in the chapter]
- [Established: the mechanism beneath "Paris," one layer deeper than Chapter 2's pattern-density account — there is no drawer, no record, no labeled cell; what exists is a "standing relationship between locations," a distributed pattern across a structure with no architect, exactly like a city that organized into neighborhoods with no zoning office ever involved. "Distributed representation" named and defined: information that exists as a pattern spread across a structure, with no single piece of it responsible for the whole]
- [Reopened, one layer deeper, deliberately still unresolved: the "knowing" question from Chapter 2's stochastic-parrots debate — now framed with full precision as functional knowledge (reliable correct output, no locatable record) vs. record-based knowledge, and explicitly named as "the *same* fault line... viewed one level further down, now that you can see the geometry underneath the fluency." The book again refuses to resolve it: "is not a question this book is going to resolve for you. It can't. Nobody currently can."]
- [New analogies deployed: "a map where every concept has an address" (central, load-bearing, caveat included); "a city built with no master plan" (distributed representation — explicitly the THIRD payoff of "novelty is scale, not kind," and flagged by the chapter itself as nearing its natural saturation point); the LEGO-kit picture extended from Chapter 2 ("picking up a piece is the same act as setting it down at a specific spot"); the live-corrected king/queen demo, whose rhetorical shape (present → check → precisely bound the correction) is now a reusable template distinct from Ch.2's "name both sides and leave open" shape]
- [Planted: a concrete, named, felt mechanical puzzle for Chapter 4 — a position on the map isn't fixed; "bank" lands near "river" in one sentence and near "interest rate" in another, the same starting point *relocated* on the fly. Something does that relocating, in real time, for every word, in every sentence — and it "lives inside an architecture this book hasn't named yet"]
- [ACT TRANSITION: this chapter closes Part 1 ("What it does"). Chapter 4 opens Part 2 ("What it knows") — a structurally ideal moment for the periodic arc-reviewer check per CLAUDE.md]
- [Bridged to: Chapter 4's question — what is the *thing*, precisely, that reads a sentence and decides, word by word, where everything in it actually belongs, this time and not the last time and not the time after that? That's the architecture. And that's where the book goes next.]

## Chapter 4 - Attention
- [Established: attention as the mechanism that relocates meaning by context — for every word, the system computes a relevance score against every other word in the sentence, recalculated fresh each time. Stated with full, unhedged confidence, explicitly calibrated to "the same kind of solid ground Chapter 2 stood on" for next-token prediction]
- [Established: the "bank" puzzle from Chapter 3 explicitly and concretely RESOLVED — the chapter walks the exact seed sentences word-by-word through the now-named mechanism and shows attention pulling "bank" toward the riverside sense in one and the financial sense in the other. A real payoff, not a gesture back at the question]
- [Established: the trophy/suitcase Winograd-schema pronoun puzzle, used to demonstrate that flipping one word ("large" → "small") flips the correct interpretation entirely — proof that the resolving mechanism must weigh the whole sentence at once]
- [Corrected — pre-emptively, before it could take root: the misconception that "attention" means the system is paying attention in a humanlike sense. Replaced with "a spreadsheet of relevance scores, recalculated from nothing every time" — cold, mechanical, exhaustive, with the resemblance to the human thing ending "exactly at the output, and not one step further in." Delivered as its own beat, with the chapter's one Sagan-style pause ("Stand back, now, and look at what you're holding...")]
- [Established: multi-head attention — many parallel relevance-computations running simultaneously, each potentially specializing in a different kind of relationship (grammar, meaning, proximity). The general fact of specialization is stated with full confidence; the specific illustrative examples are explicitly fenced off as illustrations, not a documented roster — a new precision-distinction template worth reusing]
- [Bridged to (planted callback fulfilled): the transparency-problem thread from Chapter 1, given its planned warming callback — one light sentence immediately after the multi-head explanation, in Chapter 1's even register: "we can describe, with real precision, what each of these parallel attention computations does — and we still cannot fully read out what any single one of them has specifically learned to track... the same shape... as the transparency problem this book named back at the very beginning." Fulfills the arc-reviewer's Part 1/Part 2 transition recommendation exactly as scoped]
- [Established: the context window — the bounded span of text across which attention can run at once, framed as "the stage" attention performs on; the first time the architecture's literal boundary becomes visible to the reader]
- [Established: quadratic cost — doubling the input roughly quadruples the computational cost of attention, a structural property "baked in at the same level as the transparency question from Chapter 1," not a temporary engineering snag. Anchored with concrete numbers (≈250,000 comparisons at 500 words; ≈1 million at 1,000)]
- [Established: "lost in the middle" — a real, replicated, named research finding (models perform measurably worse on information buried mid-context). Delivered via the conference-call analogy; "the model forgets" language explicitly named on the page and rejected as the wrong frame — replaced with "what changes is how reliably it gets weighed"]
- [Synthesized, without naming the retired motif: power and limit as one structural fact — the same mechanism that resolves "bank" with precision is the mechanism whose cost defines the system's hard boundaries. Echoes the *shape* of "novelty is scale, not kind" without invoking the phrase, correctly preserving it for one final, culminating appearance in Chapter 5]
- [New analogies deployed: eyes flicking back to resolve "it"/"they" (core intuition for attention, extending the hook directly); trophy/suitcase puzzle (one-shot Winograd-schema anchor); spreadsheet of relevance scores (load-bearing de-anthropomorphizing image, expected to travel into Chapters 7–8); the stage (context window); conference call (lost in the middle, vetted from research brief, explicitly avoiding "forgets" language)]
- [Retired cleanly: "a city built with no master plan" (distributed representation) was correctly NOT invoked — Chapter 4 needed no such image and reached for fresh ones instead, confirming the near-saturation flag from Chapter 3 was accurate]
- [Bridged to: Chapter 5's question — what actually changes, mechanically, when you take this same machinery and simply build it larger? Why does "bigger" stop behaving like "more of the same" and start behaving like something else entirely? Per the research brief, Chapter 5 ("Scale") is flagged HANDLE WITH EXTRA CARE — active debate territory, and the agreed final appearance of "novelty is scale, not kind"]
