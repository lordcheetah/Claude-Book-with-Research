## Chapter 1 - Learning Without Instructions

- [Established: the training loop — guess, measure (loss function), adjust (gradient descent), repeat — "a skier descending a mountain in fog" analogy, explicitly flagged in-text to resurface in later chapters]

- [Planted: the transparency problem is structural, not a flaw or oversight — a system that finds its own patterns will necessarily hold patterns nobody can fully read back out; "powerful" and "hard to explain" reframed as one property seen from two angles — seed for Chapter 7]

## Chapter 2 - Reading as Prediction
- [Established: text reaches and leaves the system as tokens, not words — "a kit of interlocking LEGO-style pieces" analogy explains tokenization concretely and corrects the "strawberry" letter-counting misconception in the same stroke (a system may never see "strawberry" as eight letters, only as whatever pieces its kit breaks it into)]
- [Established: next-token prediction as the entire pretraining objective — stated with full, unhedged confidence per the research brief's instruction that this ground is settled. Not "understand," not "know what's true," not "be helpful." One task, repeated an almost incomprehensible number of times, across nearly everything humans have digitized and written down]
- [Established: the "Paris" mechanism — fluent, correct-sounding output is produced via statistical pattern density, not stored-fact lookup or retrieval. "Paris" isn't an answer the system looked up; it's the single most likely next token, by an overwhelming margin, given a pattern repeated across thousands of independent sources]
- [Corrected: "the model looks things up / retrieves facts like a search engine or database" — dismantled directly and by name via the "Paris" walkthrough, replaced with the actual mechanism (pattern density, not retrieval)]
- [Named fairly, deliberately left open: the "stochastic parrots" debate — one serious camp (Bender, Mitchell, et al., 2021) holding that fluency is not understanding, full stop; another holding that this framing increasingly strains against what these systems visibly do. The book explicitly commits to NOT resolving this — "the result *looks like* knowing... nobody currently knows" — and later chapters (5, 8) must preserve this live, unresolved, both-serious framing]
- [Planted: the structural sounds-true/is-true gap — a system optimized purely for "sounds right" has no built-in way to distinguish that from "is right," identified as a *necessary* structural consequence of the training objective (not a flaw, not half-finished engineering), stated in the same even register Chapter 1 used for the transparency problem — seed for Chapter 6 (hallucination)]
- [New analogies deployed: phone autocomplete "scaled roughly a billion-fold" (the chapter's anchor, doing for Ch.2 what the spam filter did for Ch.1); crayon-drawing-of-a-coastline vs. satellite-photograph (the qualitative leap from small to large, channeling "novelty is scale, not kind"); LEGO-kit of interlocking pieces (tokenization, load-bearing for Chapter 3); the million-dial control panel from Ch.1 recalled and explicitly multiplied to billions]
- [Bridged to: Chapter 3's question — a pile of correctly-predicted tokens isn't, by itself, meaning; producing "Paris" in the right place isn't the same as the concept of Paris meaning anything to anything. So: if prediction is the *task* — and we now know, in detail, that it is — what is the system actually *holding*, internally, that makes its predictions this good? What does "the capital of France is Paris" look like inside something that has never seen a map, a city, or a capital of any kind?]

## Chapter 4 - Attention

- [Established: the "bank" puzzle from Chapter 3 explicitly and concretely RESOLVED — the chapter walks the exact seed sentences word-by-word through the now-named mechanism and shows attention pulling "bank" toward the riverside sense in one and the financial sense in the other. A real payoff, not a gesture back at the question]
- [Established: the trophy/suitcase Winograd-schema pronoun puzzle, used to demonstrate that flipping one word ("large" → "small") flips the correct interpretation entirely — proof that the resolving mechanism must weigh the whole sentence at once]
- [Corrected — pre-emptively, before it could take root: the misconception that "attention" means the system is paying attention in a humanlike sense. Replaced with "a spreadsheet of relevance scores, recalculated from nothing every time" — cold, mechanical, exhaustive, with the resemblance to the human thing ending "exactly at the output, and not one step further in." Delivered as its own beat, with the chapter's one Sagan-style pause ("Stand back, now, and look at what you're holding...")]
- [Established: multi-head attention — many parallel relevance-computations running simultaneously, each potentially specializing in a different kind of relationship (grammar, meaning, proximity). The general fact of specialization is stated with full confidence; the specific illustrative examples are explicitly fenced off as illustrations, not a documented roster — a new precision-distinction template worth reusing]
- [Bridged to (planted callback fulfilled): the transparency-problem thread from Chapter 1, given its planned warming callback — one light sentence immediately after the multi-head explanation, in Chapter 1's even register: "we can describe, with real precision, what each of these parallel attention computations does — and we still cannot fully read out what any single one of them has specifically learned to track... the same shape... as the transparency problem this book named back at the very beginning." - [Established: the context window — the bounded span of text across which attention can run at once, framed as "the stage" attention performs on; the first time the architecture's literal boundary becomes visible to the reader]
- [Established: quadratic cost — doubling the input roughly quadruples the computational cost of attention, a structural property "baked in at the same level as the transparency question from Chapter 1," not a temporary engineering snag. Anchored with concrete numbers (≈250,000 comparisons at 500 words; ≈1 million at 1,000)]
- [Established: "lost in the middle" — a real, replicated, named research finding (models perform measurably worse on information buried mid-context). ## Chapter 5 - Scale
- [Established: the parameters/data/compute distinction — "bigger" is not one dial but three, and they must scale together or the result underperforms; stated as architecture-level fact, the floor the rest of the chapter stands on. Returning analogy: the Chapter 1 million-dial control panel, explicitly multiplied by a thousand]
- [Established, full confidence, calibrated to the same tier as Chapter 2's next-token-prediction claim: scaling laws — performance improves predictably and graphably as scale increases, a finding that has held since 2020 and "survived years of subsequent attempts to break it"]
- [Established: the Chinchilla finding (DeepMind, 2022) — model size and training-data size must grow in matched proportion for a fixed compute budget, refining (not contradicting) scaling laws; a second deployment of the "present the simple version, then sharpen it" template Chapter 3 introduced. Complicated immediately and honestly by a real-world economic wrinkle: companies like Meta deliberately train smaller models on more data than the formula calls optimal, for inference-cost reasons (the LLaMA example)]
- [Established: emergent capabilities — named, exemplified (in-context learning, multi-step reasoning), and given a concrete approximate threshold (~100B parameters) at which capabilities that simply did not exist below that scale begin reliably appearing above it. Both camps in the ensuing debate AGREE these capability differences are real — what's disputed is only how to interpret their appearance pattern]
- [THE CHAPTER'S CENTRAL CRAFT EVENT — presented two-sided, genuinely unresolved, and explicitly left open: the emergence-interpretation debate. Schaeffer, Miranda, and Koyejo (2023) argue apparent capability "cliffs" are measurement artifacts manufactured by coarse, all-or-nothing scoring (illustrated via a coarse-thermometer analogy); Du and colleagues (2025) argue the sharpness of these jumps tracks a real, measurable internal threshold that persists even under continuous scoring. The chapter gives both equal room, equal specificity, and equal respect, and explicitly declines to crown a winner: "nobody knows... the disagreement itself has *moved*, rather than resolved." Verified by the nonfiction-reviewer to contain no false-balance failure modes (no last-word effect, no recency bias toward the 2025 paper, no ordering bias) — the single most carefully fair piece of writing in the book to date]
- [Corrected, pre-emptively: the misconception that "emergent" means something mystical or spontaneous happened inside the system. The same ordinary training process that produced everything else in this book produced these capabilities too — what's disputed is never *whether* training caused them, only *how their arrival became visible*. A direct structural echo of Chapter 4's pre-emptive correction of "attention"]
- [Named fairly, deliberately bounded: a brief, explicitly-fenced kinship drawn between the emergence-interpretation debate and the stochastic-parrots debate from Chapters 2–3 — called "cousins," not the same argument, with the distinction actively maintained rather than blurred]
- [Demonstrated, in miniature: the Kosinski theory-of-mind controversy (2024), explicitly marked as a different and smaller kind of dispute than the emergence debate above ("does this capability exist at all," not "how did its arrival become visible") — giving the reader a concrete, ground-level taste of what active scientific contestation looks like up close]
- [RETIRED — permanently, explicitly, on the page: "novelty is scale, not kind," in its fourth and final appearance. Earned by showing that both the settled phenomena (scaling laws, Chinchilla) and the strange phenomena (emergence) are produced by the exact same machinery, just at different scale — the one point both warring camps in the emergence debate actually agree on. The chapter closes the door explicitly: "You will not hear it again after this page... Consider it retired." DO NOT reintroduce this phrase in any later chapter]
- [New analogies deployed: the hand-drawn coastline sketch vs. satellite photograph (the chapter's central organizing image, a structural cousin of Chapter 2's crayon-drawing analogy but built to interrogate exactly how literal the "clearer picture" framing is); the coarse thermometer marked only every ten degrees (one-shot, mechanically precise illustration of how coarse measurement manufactures cliffs out of slopes — explicitly scoped to avoid overclaiming, and elegantly reused in the chapter's own closing synthesis to characterize BOTH camps, not just the skeptics)]

This arc is now CLOSED]
- [Established, named and dated with full, verified confidence: the Kalai et al. (2025) grading-incentive account ("Why Language Models Hallucinate," Kalai, Nachum, Vempala, and Zhang, OpenAI) — landed via the multiple-choice-student/grading-system analogy (a test where a wrong answer scores the same as a blank teaches confident guessing over honest uncertainty, through the grading alone). Explicitly and precisely distinguished from the "Paris" mechanism as a complementary second layer, not a competing explanation: "These aren't competing explanations. They're complementary engines, stacked." The mechanism/incentive separation is the chapter's most important single piece of explanatory architecture, and it held with zero drift]
- [RETURNED — the skier-in-fog analogy's SECOND of its promised "more than once" appearances (planted Ch.1, explicitly flagged there to "resurface, in different shapes, more than once"). - [Corrected — back to back, in the same plain register Ch.4 used to dismantle "attention," and delivered as genuine demonstrations rather than assertions: (1) hallucination is NOT lying — a lie requires intention to deceive, a belief formed and then betrayed, and nothing in any mechanism this book has built resembles that; calling it lying "smuggles in an entire cast of characters that was never there"; (2) hallucination is NOT rare — it is a structural feature of how these systems generate any text at all, present in every output, simply more visible in some cases than others. - [Delivered — the book's first fully ACTIONABLE payload, the practical center of gravity five chapters of mechanism-building were explicitly building toward: concrete risk-stratification guidance using the sounds-true/is-true overlap as a map. Thick ground (broad, well-documented, low-specificity — general explanations, common knowledge) runs comparatively trustworthy; thin ground (citations, exact dates, named individuals, statistics, niche/recent facts) is where the gap opens widest — and counterintuitively, the more specific and confident an answer sounds in exactly this territory, the MORE verification it deserves, "because specificity is precisely the costume fabrication wears best." Replaces both easy postures ("don't trust it" / "trust it") with: know what kind of ground you're standing on, verify in proportion to how thin it runs]

## Chapter 7 - The Black Box
- [Established: opens cold inside Chapter 6's inherited question — extending its pre-loaded "wire that doesn't glow" image into the chapter's own hook, the car-engine comparison: an engine is a machine you can watch *work* (pistons, timing, the whole cycle, comprehensible by looking); an LLM has no equivalent surface — "not a hidden one, an absent one." Lands the chapter's organizing question in the book's own register: not "can we explain what these things do" but "if you wanted to check, right now, whether this system is standing on thick ground or thin the moment it answers — what, mechanically, would you even look at?"]
- [Corrected — cleanly and early, the single biggest popular misconception about this subject, with the same load-bearing precision Ch.4 brought to "attention" and Ch.6 brought to "lying": "nobody understands how these models work" is false. The architecture (code, math, training procedure — token to vector to attention pattern to probability distribution) is completely, thoroughly understood, because humans wrote every line of it; published, taught, rebuilt independently by competing labs. What remains genuinely hard is a different question entirely: what the system *learned to do* with that architecture — actual patterns encoded across billions of adjustable numbers, "not tractable" to read out, in the field's own unflinching self-assessment, not an excuse]
- [Established: mechanistic interpretability named plainly, once, as exactly what it is — a search for *structure* (recurring patterns, identifiable sub-routines, internal landmarks), explicitly NOT a search for "the model's mind," declining the anthropomorphizing this book has refused for six straight chapters. Framed as patient, ground-level detective work — setting up the reader to be genuinely surprised by what such a search actually found]
- [Established — the chapter's central positive-evidence beat, given real, unhurried space and landed as settled-tier, fully citable, no-hedging evidence: Anthropic's "Scaling Monosemanticity" (May 2024) — using sparse autoencoders, researchers searching inside Claude 3 Sonnet found millions of distinct, separable, *nameable* internal "features," including one that corresponded unmistakably to the Golden Gate Bridge. Researchers amplified that feature deliberately and watched behavior shift predictably — for 24 hours in May 2024, the public could talk to "Golden Gate Claude," which answered ordinary questions with lines like the verbatim-quoted "I am the Golden Gate Bridge… my physical form is the iconic bridge itself." Landed explicitly as "what real progress looks like when it stops being a promise and becomes a citation" — the single best answer to anyone who thinks the inside of these systems is permanently fog with nothing anyone could point to]
- [Widened — from one lab's demo to a field-wide convergence signal, the same "more than one source arrives independently" pattern this book has used before (Ch.5's scaling-law consensus, Ch.6's RAG consensus): Anthropic's May 2025 circuit-tracing tools and ongoing Circuits Updates describe *recurring* internal sub-routines with plain mechanical names — "induction heads," "copy heads," "previous-token heads" — showing up across different models from different teams, with comparable work underway at OpenAI, DeepMind, and academic labs]
- [PIVOT — the designed alternative to "Stand back," successfully landed for a SECOND consecutive chapter via callback-and-extension rather than synthesis-from-above: "Here is what that demonstration proves: that a specific, identifiable pattern exists, that touching it changes the output, and that the change runs in a direction researchers could predict. Here is what it does not prove: that anyone could take any single sentence this system produces... and trace, with confidence, the specific internal causes that produced exactly that sentence and no other." Drew the sharp boundary immediately, then backed it with the most efficient possible evidence — Anthropic's own July 2025 self-assessment, quoted verbatim: its interpretability findings are "like those of a colleague sharing some thoughts or preliminary experiments... rather than a mature paper," landed as exactly the kind of self-correcting honesty this book has asked the reader, from page one, to recognize as a strength]
- [Corrected — the second misconception, explicitly marked as RHYMING with the first (beat 6/beat 9 deliberate echo): saliency/attribution methods (heat-maps, highlighted words — "here's what it was looking at") are useful, limited, and sometimes actively misleading, landed via the heat-map/spotlight-vs-play analogy (knowing where the light fell ≠ knowing what caused the outcome) and backed by a 2024 systematic review of 68 user studies finding a genuinely mixed picture — sometimes helps, sometimes no effect, sometimes actively misleads. A colorful visualization FEELS like an explanation; at best it's a clue, at worst it manufactures the feeling of a shown cause where no causal claim has been demonstrated — the correlation/causation distinction this book has been building the reader's ear for since Ch.5]
- [PAID OFF IN FULL — the transparency-problem thread, explicitly marked on the page as a kept promise the way Ch.6 marked the sounds-true/is-true return: "Here it is, picked back up exactly as promised — the cost side of the very same coin Chapter 1 first turned over."
