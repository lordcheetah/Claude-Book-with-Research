# Back-Cover Blurb — Thinking Machines: How AI Actually Works

Somewhere in the gap between the moment you ask an AI a question and the moment its answer appears, something makes a decision — and you almost never ask how. Most explanations of what's happening in that gap land in one of two bad places: "it's just statistics" (true, incomplete, and somehow still unhelpful) or "it's basically magic" (which is another way of saying *we don't understand it, so let's stop trying*).

*Thinking Machines* takes a third path. Starting from the simplest possible question — how does any machine learn a pattern from examples instead of being told a rule? — it builds, piece by piece, to a clear picture of how large language models actually read, represent, predict, and occasionally lie with total confidence. Along the way it covers attention, embeddings, scale effects, the black-box problem researchers still can't fully solve, and the genuinely open questions — about alignment, about machine consciousness, about where this is all heading — that the field's most careful people are still arguing about in print.

It does not predict the future. It does not tell you what to think about it. What it hands you instead is something that travels: a working habit for sorting any claim about this technology — or any other — into what's settled, what's honestly contested, and what's just speculation wearing a confident voice.

Understanding how the trick works doesn't make it less impressive. It makes the wonder sharper.
