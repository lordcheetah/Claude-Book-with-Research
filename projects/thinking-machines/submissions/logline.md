# Logline — Thinking Machines: How AI Actually Works

## Primary
A clear-eyed guided tour through how large language models actually work — from the first weight that adjusts itself to the questions nobody can yet answer — that leaves readers not with a stockpile of facts, but with a portable habit for telling what's settled, what's argued, and what's just confidently guessed.

## Alternate (shorter, ~30 words)
Most explanations of AI land in one of two bad places — "it's just statistics" or "it's basically magic." This book takes a third path: it shows you the mechanism, and trusts you with what's still unknown.

## Alternate (thesis-forward)
Understanding how AI works doesn't make it less impressive. It makes the wonder more specific — and gives you a tool for spotting confident-sounding nonsense anywhere it appears, including in the very last chapter of this book.

---//
*Note on adaptation*: The skill's logline template is built for protagonist-driven fiction ("[protagonist] must [goal] before [stakes]"). This is an argument-driven nonfiction explainer with no protagonist — the throughline is the **reader's transformation** (naive wonder → equipped skepticism), not a character's arc. These three options foreground that transformation at different altitudes: the first is structural ("guided tour... leaves readers with"), the second is the book's own stated provocation (the two bad explanations it rejects), and the third is the payoff stated in the book's own closing register. Recommend the first for query letters (it names the audience's actual experience — being stuck between two unsatisfying explanations) and the third for jacket copy (it ends on the book's own voice).
