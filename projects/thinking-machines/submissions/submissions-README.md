# Submissions — Notes for the Author

These four documents were generated against the **finished, finalized manuscript** (all 8 chapters, gated and archived in `story/chapters/`), cross-checked against `story/synopsis.md` (the original pre-writing pitch), `story/plan.md`, `bible/style.md`, and the post-completion full-manuscript arc review (`.work/thinking-machines/arc-review-full-manuscript.md`).

## Adaptation note — this is nonfiction, not fiction

The `synopsis-writer` skill's templates are written for protagonist-driven fiction: loglines structured around "[protagonist] must [goal] before [stakes]," query synopses that "include the ending" of a plot, query letters built around comp-title positioning for a character's journey. *Thinking Machines* has no protagonist, no plot, and no traditional resolution — its throughline is the **reader's transformation** (naive wonder about AI → a portable, working habit for sorting claims into settled / contested / speculative). Every document below substitutes that transformation for a character arc, and substitutes the book's **thesis and closing argument** for a plot's "ending."

## What was generated

- **`logline.md`** — Three options at different altitudes (structural / provocation-forward / payoff-forward), since there's no single "protagonist + stakes" formula to anchor on. Recommends the first for query letters, the third for jacket copy.
- **`back-cover-blurb.md`** — Built from the strongest framing already present in the manuscript itself (the "two bad explanations" the book explicitly rejects, drawn near-verbatim from Chapter 8's own language) rather than invented marketing copy — this book is unusually self-aware about its own pitch, so the blurb leans on its own voice.
- **`query-synopsis.md`** — Follows the actual finished structure (four movements: Ch.1–3 settled ground, Ch.4–5 the pivot, Ch.6 the practical payload, Ch.7–8 open territory and the capstone) rather than the originally-planned structure, and ends with a "How it resolves" section that names the sorting-habit payoff as the book's actual ending — the nonfiction equivalent of the required "ending paragraph."
- **`query-letter-opening.md`** — Three paragraphs (hook / expanded pitch / comp titles). Comp titles chosen: *The Alignment Problem* (Brian Christian, 2020) and *Why Machines Learn* (Anil Ananthaswamy, 2024) as primary — both recent, well-regarded, general-audience books in the same "real mechanism, no metaphor-as-substitute" lane, neither a chart-topping bestseller that would make the comparison feel presumptuous; *Calling Bullshit* (Bergstrom & West, 2020) as a secondary comp specifically for the book's most distinctive feature, that its payload is a transferable skill rather than a stockpile of facts.

## A structural divergence worth knowing about

`story/plan.md` originally specified a standalone ~1,500-word **Introduction** ("The Magic Trick," opening on a specific impressive AI output) and a standalone ~1,500-word **Conclusion** ("Why This Matters") bookending the eight numbered chapters.

**Neither was written as a separate chapter.** Chapter 1 opens directly on the spam-filter hook (a different — and, on reflection, stronger — opening than the planned "impressive AI output" framing, because it starts from something the reader already unthinkingly trusts rather than something engineered to impress). Chapter 8's closing movement fully absorbs the planned Conclusion's argument: the callback to the book's opening, the "disposition not a position" framing, and the turn toward better questions all land on the page inside Chapter 8.

The post-completion arc review confirmed this was the right call structurally — both merges tighten the book rather than leaving it feeling incomplete — and `plan.md` has already been annotated to record the Conclusion merge as intentional. **The query synopsis above reflects the actual eight-chapter structure**, not the originally planned ten-section one. If an agent or editor asks about the book's structure, the honest answer is "eight chapters, fully self-bookending" — not "eight chapters plus intro and conclusion."

## What to customize before sending

- **Comp titles**: swap for titles specific to an agent's actual list if known — the three chosen here were selected for broad appeal across popular-science/tech-nonfiction agents generally, not for any particular submission target.
- **Logline**: pick one of the three based on context (query letter vs. jacket copy vs. pitch deck) — see the note at the bottom of `logline.md`.
- **Word count framing**: 25,500 words is a short nonfiction work — closer to a long-form essay collection or a "Brief History of..."-style compact explainer than a standard 70k-90k nonfiction book. If targeting agents/imprints that expect longer manuscripts, the query letter may want to frame this proactively as a deliberate choice (the book's own voice models exactly this kind of restraint — "not technically comprehensive, deliberately selective for clarity," per `story/synopsis.md`) rather than let it read as unfinished.
